RESEARCH DOSSIERS

M073: an unequal-mass, unequal-diagonal Sidorenko margin on an ordered cone

November 1, 2026. Scoped mathematical note under claim #151, building on the already merged regular two-block proof. This note proves a real-parameter inequality, not just 85 sampled cases. Originality and specialist independent review remain unverified. The main mathematical input is the classical Harris–FKG positive-association theorem for independent coordinates (Grimmett, Probability on Graphs, §4.2). The adjacent-edge pairing has a classical line-graph interpretation; the existence of perfect matchings for connected even-order claw-free graphs is due to Sumner / Las Vergnas, independently of this project. Here we give an explicit, smaller constructive certificate rather than appealing to that theorem.

The fixed pattern is the exact 35-vertex, 66-edge bipartite incidence graph (H) of the 22 triples on 13 points from the 2026 OpenAI Sidorenko manuscript, with a pinned source table at data/reference/sidorenko_faces.json. The external manuscript's general/finite host claim remains a different, unreviewed question, not settled here.

Proposition — arbitrary block masses, ordered 2×2 weights#

Let (a,b,c,q\in[0,1]). Give the first of two vertex blocks mass (q) and the other mass (1-q), and let their symmetric edge-weight matrix be

[ W=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\b&c\end{pmatrix}. ]

Assume either (a\ge b\ge c) or (c\ge b\ge a) (the ordered/monotone cone, inclusive of its boundary). Put

[ \begin{aligned} d_0&=qa+(1-q)b, & d_1&=qb+(1-q)c,\ p&=qd_0+(1-q)d_1 =q^2a+2q(1-q)b+(1-q)^2c,\ V&=q(1-q)(d_0-d_1)^2 =q(1-q)\left[q(a-b)+(1-q)(b-c)\right]^2. \end{aligned} ]

Then

[ \boxed{\qquad t(H,W)\ \ge\ (p^2+V)^{33}\ \ge\ p^{66}.\qquad} \tag{1} ]

Both inequalities are strict if (0<q<1) and (a\ne c). Equality holds when (q\in\{0,1\}) or (a=b=c). This includes sparse and zero graphons, and makes no equal-diagonal ((a=c)) or equal-mass ((q=1/2)) assumption.

This is a newly derived application in this repository of standard positive association to a source-specific edge-pair certificate. It should not be advertised as an independently novel general theorem. For any graph with an adjacent pairing of its (2k) edges, exactly the same argument yields (t(F,W)\ge(p^2+V)^k) on the ordered cone. Consequently, the method is not exclusive to (H); its role here is to establish a specific quantitative exclusion within the active counterexample search.

Proof#

Part A: 66 exact edges can be partitioned into 33 wedges. For each of the source's face triples (f_j) make edges ((i,13+j)) for its three points (i). The resulting 66-edge graph is connected. The committed 33-wedge cover partitions those 66 distinct edges into 33 pairs, each pair sharing a center vertex. It is independently validated against the separately pinned face table: every source edge occurs once and only once, and each wedge's two edges meet its declared center.

The cover is not guessed: a deterministic postorder spanning-tree construction independently regenerates the same 33 groups. Root the connected graph, visit all nonroot vertices in reverse breadth-first order, and at each vertex pair all still-free incident edges except the parent edge. If their number is odd, include the reserved parent edge. The root necessarily has even residual degree because the graph started with an even number of edges and every previous step consumed pairs. Thus no edge remains unmatched. The source JSON and algorithm also receive independent tampering tests.

Part B: positive association with no appeal to bipartiteness. Give each vertex of (H) an independent block label (X_v), with (\Pr(X_v=0)=q). If (a\ge b\ge c), every edge function (W(X_u,X_v)) is coordinatewise nonincreasing in the labels (0<1); if (c\ge b\ge a), it is nondecreasing. Each wedge factor is the product of two nonnegative edge functions and is therefore coordinatewise monotone in the same direction. Harris's product-measure inequality applies to the 33 wedge factors (G_1,\ldots,G_{33}):

[ t(H,W)=\mathbb{E}\prod_{j=1}^{33}G_j \ge \prod_{j=1}^{33}\mathbb{E}G_j. ]

Within wedge ((xy,xz)), conditioning on its center label (X_x) makes (X_y,X_z) independent, whence

[ \mathbb{E}G_j =\mathbb{E}[d(X_x)^2] =p^2+\operatorname{Var}(d(X_x)) =p^2+q(1-q)(d_0-d_1)^2. ]

Multiplication proves both non-strict inequalities in (1).

Part C: strictness. The first two explicitly checked wedges have center vertex (0) and edge pairs (\{(0,13),(0,14)\}) and (\{(0,15),(0,16)\}). Their four leaf vertices are all distinct. Group these two wedges as one monotone factor, then apply Harris to that factor and the other 31 wedge factors. The grouped pair has expectation (\mathbb{E}d(X_0)^4), strictly exceeding ((\mathbb{E}d(X_0)^2)^2) when (0<q<1) and (d_0\ne d_1). Indeed the difference equals (q(1-q)(d_0^2-d_1^2)^2). Ordered nonnegative (a,b,c) imply (d_0\ne d_1) exactly when (a\ne c), because (d_0-d_1=q(a-b)+(1-q)(b-c)), whose summands have the same sign. The strict inequality follows. When (q=0,1), the support has only one class; when (a=b=c), the graphon is constant. Both yield equality. (\square)

Exact machine audit and independent falsifiers#

The fixed pattern is SHA-256 pinned in data/reference/sidorenko_faces.json. The separate JSON wedge-cover witness is not the sole authority: the independent validation route checks its 66-edge multiset, centers, distinctness and source hash; the other route constructs it afresh from a rooted graph traversal. Tests alter the witness by deleting, repeating, reversing and changing edges and centers, and require fail-closed behavior.

For a rational graphon use integer mass numerators (q=P/Q) and integer edge entries (a/D,b/D,c/D). The independent face-based evaluator returns (T) such that

[ t(H,W)=\frac{T}{Q^{35}D^{66}}. ]

Writing

[ B=P\big(Pa+(Q-P)b\big)^2+ (Q-P)\big(Pb+(Q-P)c\big)^2, ]

the exact cleared-denominator test is (TQ^{64}\ge B^{33}). This is integer arithmetic; no floating-point comparison or graph sampling. The identity (BQ=(P^2a+2P(Q-P)b+(Q-P)^2c)^2 +P(Q-P)[P(a-b)+(Q-P)(b-c)]^2) separately checks the covariance normalization.

The checker replays 85 preregistered test cases across all ordered triples (a,b,c\in\{0,1/2,1\}) and five rational masses (q\in\{0,1/3,1/2,2/3,1\}), including the zero and constant boundaries. Four of those cases are independently recounted via 35-variable pairwise-edge factor elimination, rather than the face-based 13-point summation. On tiny independent patterns both exact algorithms agree with direct brute-force enumeration of all vertex labels. Negative controls reject nonmonotone matrices, invalid probabilities, a malformed wedge and a changed source triple; they do not assert that Sidorenko fails in the nonmonotone region.

Scope and next falsifier#

This result excludes the entire ordered cone of two-block graphons for all block masses as witnesses against M073. It is substantially broader than our prior balanced equal-diagonal theorem but does not contain it: antimonotone off-diagonal patterns (for example (a=c<b)) lie outside the ordered cone and were handled differently in M073_REGULAR2_CYCLE_MARGIN.md.

The rest of the three-parameter space is still open to attack: (b<\min(a,c)) or (b>\max(a,c)), including unequal diagonals and nonuniform masses. Search those nonmonotone regions for rational negative density gaps, or obtain the explicit finite host claimed in the upstream preprint. Any discovery must be independently recounted against the same SHA-256-pinned (H). This restricted proposition has not passed an external novelty audit, specialist independent review, or the unrestricted Solved gate.
