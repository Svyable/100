RESEARCH DOSSIERS

M019 — exact failure of the universal fixed shifts 3 and 7

2026-11-01 · Work item O-M019-01 · claim #121

Result type: a rigorous, bounded negative result about two proposed first denominators. This is not a novel theorem, an Erdős–Straus counterexample, a proof of the conjecture, or an independently reviewed result.

Exact statement and proof#

Fix positive integers n,c with 4 | n+c, and insist on the first denominator x=(n+c)/4. Set m=nx. Then positive integers y,z satisfy

4/n = 1/x + 1/y + 1/z

if and only if a positive divisor d | m² exists with

d ≡ -m (mod c),      m²/d ≡ -m (mod c).

Indeed, subtracting 1/x gives 1/y+1/z=c/m, hence (cy-m)(cz-m)=m². Both factors are positive: if cy≤m, then 1/y≥c/m contradicts the positive remaining summand. Conversely, given the two congruences, y=(m+d)/c and z=(m+m²/d)/c are positive integers and satisfy the original identity. If gcd(m,c)=1, the second congruence follows from the first, but the checker tests both directly.

For n=1129, trial division through floor(sqrt(1129))=33 proves primality, and 1129≡1 (mod 24).

  • Shift 3: x=283 (prime), m=1129·283. Both prime factors are 1 (mod 3), so every positive divisor of m² is 1 (mod 3). But the required divisor must be -m≡2 (mod 3). Impossible.
  • Shift 7: x=284=2²·71, m=1129·284. The prime-factor residues modulo 7 are 1129≡2, 2≡2, 71≡1. Every divisor of m² therefore lies in the multiplicative subgroup {1,2,4} modulo 7. Yet m≡1 (mod 7), so the required divisor residue is 6, outside that subgroup. Impossible.

Thus neither 4x-n=3 nor 4x-n=7 can work at the prime n=1129. In particular, a universally valid positive-integer identity for all primes n≡1 (mod 24) under either fixed shift cannot exist. This directly falsifies the universal wording of O-M019-01, not the underlying Erdős–Straus conjecture.

The independent divisor-enumeration path checks every divisor of m² for each shift, with no numerical tolerance or bounded-denominator search. A separate prime-factor subgroup proof gives the obstruction without enumerating divisors. The checker also exhausts primes n≡1 (mod 24) below 1129, finding at least one of these two shifts for each; 1129 is the first joint failure in that bounded scan, not a new global number-theoretic bound.

Essential positive control#

The same prime does have an Egyptian-fraction representation using shift 11:

4/1129 = 1/285 + 1/29260 + 1/99103620.

The checker verifies this as an exact fractions.Fraction identity. It also accepts shift 3 at n=97 and shift 7 at n=73, guarding against a vacuous all-negative test.

Reproduce and falsify#

python3 scripts/straus_shift_gate.py --check
python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -p 'test_straus_shift_gate.py' -v

Eight adversarial tests cover prime-factor certificates, the two independent modular obstructions, a positive witness for 1129, two other positive controls, brute divisor comparison for small instances, first-joint-failure enumeration, and invalid inputs. Mutation targets include a spurious divisor residue, a missing prime factor, and a false assertion that no decomposition exists.

Boundary: Only the two forced first denominators are excluded. Other values of x remain available; no conjecture-wide result follows. This does not meet the original obligation's constructive acceptance criterion and does not discharge it. The appropriate next step is to re-scope the obligation away from universal fixed shifts and toward a genuinely new restricted family or a new analytic mechanism.
