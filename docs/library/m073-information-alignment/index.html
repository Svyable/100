RESEARCH DOSSIERS

M073: aligning binary labels, parity constraints and graphon densities

November 1, 2026. Work item O-M073-02, claim #195.

Result: the complete even-subgraph polynomial of the pinned 35-vertex, 66-edge incidence graph is now computed by two different exact algorithms. The newly recorded coefficients strengthen the previous quantitative bound. This is a source-specific calculation using classical mathematics, with independent mathematical and historical-novelty review still outstanding. It does not resolve Sidorenko or produce a counterexample host.

Align meanings before manipulating symbols#

Let H be the incidence graph of the pinned 22 triples on 13 points. The two graphon classes have mass 1/2 and block matrix W=((a,b),(b,a)), with a,b in [0,1]. Put p=(a+b)/2, r=(a-b)/(a+b) when p>0.

RepresentationObjectExactly preserved quantity
GraphonProduct of 66 edge weights averaged over vertex labelst(H,W)/p^66
Spin systemIndependent uniform signs at all 35 verticesE product over uv of (1+r sigma_u sigma_v)
Binary linear codeKernel of the incidence matrix over F_2Sum of r^weight over all codewords
Hypergraph coloringBinary labels on the 13 points; A monochromatic triplesE[(1+3r^2)^A (1-r^2)^(22-A)]

These are equalities with explicit normalization, not analogies between similar notation. The spin and parity identities are classical high-temperature/Fourier expansions; see Friedli–Velenik, Section 3.5. The wider code/partition-function connection is classical too: Forney, Codes on graphs: Duality and MacWilliams identities. The source-specific complete spectrum below is the new-to-project output; these citations are not evidence of its historical novelty.

Exact representation theorem#

The following identity applies to the incidence graph of any finite family of triples, including repeated triples as distinct face vertices. Let n be the number of points and f the number of faces. For a point-sign assignment s, let A(s) be the number of faces with three identical signs. Then

[ Z_H(r):=\sum_{S\subseteq E(H):\,\partial S=0}r^{|S|} =2^{-n}\sum_{s\in\{-1,1\}^n} (1+3r^2)^{A(s)}(1-r^2)^{f-A(s)}. \tag{1} ]

Here partial S is the vector of vertex degrees modulo two; every edge subset has coefficient one before collecting by cardinality. Equation (1) is a polynomial identity for all real r, independent of graphon restrictions.

Proof. Expand the spin product over all edges. Independence and E[sigma^k]=0 for odd k, 1 for even k, annihilate exactly the subsets with nonzero degree parity. This gives the left side. Alternatively integrate the sign tau at a face {u,v,w} first:

[ \tfrac12\sum_{\tau=\pm1} (1+r\tau s_u)(1+r\tau s_v)(1+r\tau s_w) =1+r^2(s_us_v+s_us_w+s_vs_w). ]

The parenthesized sum is 3 when the three signs agree and -1 otherwise. Face signs are independent conditional on point signs. Multiplying these f factors and then averaging over all point signs proves (1). Finally W(s,t)=p(1+rst), so t(H,W)=p^(3f) Z_H(r) for the stated graphons.

There is a complementary direct recurrence. At each degree-three face, an even edge subset chooses either no edges or one of its three edge pairs. Track the XOR of the selected point endpoints and the number k of chosen pairs. Starting from state (0,0) with count one, each face adds the four possible transitions. At the end the counts in parity state zero are exactly the coefficients of r^(2k). Every selection has a unique sequence of local choices, so this recurrence neither omits nor duplicates a subset. It uses no spin transform or cancellation and checks every coefficient.

Complete pinned spectrum and consequences#

Writing x=r^2, Z_H(r)=sum from k=0 to 22 of c_k x^k, the coefficient vector is

[1, 0, 33, 88, 823, 4232, 24831, 127584,
 603274, 2540960, 9417578, 30237520, 83066126,
 192776048, 373654910, 599077600, 785645157,
 830015520, 690493733, 436109080, 196772043,
 56595016, 7805139]

The machine-readable certificate also records the full histogram of A and the source digest. This recovers the weight distribution of all 2^32 = 4,294,967,296 even subgraphs from 2^13 = 8,192 point-label assignments. These are state counts, not a claim of a measured 524,288-fold runtime speedup. No individual enumeration of billions of subgraphs is performed. The previous face-elimination engine already exploited the 13-point structure for numeric evaluation; this work extracts the entire polynomial and adds a combinatorial coefficient check.

Because every c_k is nonnegative, for all graphons in the stated family,

[ t(H,W)\ge p^{66}\bigl(1+33r^4+88r^6+ 823r^8+4232r^{10}+24831r^{12}\bigr). \tag{2} ]

For p>0 and a unequal to b, (2) is strict since c_7=127584>0. The displayed bound strictly improves the earlier 1+33r^4+88r^6 bound whenever a differs from b. At a=b it equals p^66. When a=b=0, treat the case separately: the density and bound are both zero, and r is undefined.

Three additional concrete deductions illustrate what the alignment reveals:

  1. 823 is not the eight-cycle count. It splits as 361 simple eight-cycles plus 462 distinct unions of two edge-disjoint four-cycles. Such unions may share a point vertex. A separate cycle DFS and edge-set census verify this split; confusing cycle counts with even-subgraph counts would miss it.
  2. The leading coefficient 7,805,139 counts the even subgraphs containing two edges at every face (44 edges total). Larger even subgraphs cannot exist, because each of the 22 degree-three face vertices has selected degree zero or two.
  3. There are 96 labeled two-colorings of the 13 points with no monochromatic face (48 up to global color reversal), from histogram entry A=0. This is a coloring fact about the pinned hypergraph, not a Sidorenko counterexample.

Checks, limits and next useful transfer#

Run:

python3 scripts/m073_information_alignment.py --check
python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -p 'test_m073_information_alignment.py' -v
python3 scripts/regen.py --check

Validation compares all coefficients from the spin-histogram transform and the parity-state recurrence, and separately evaluates six exact weight pairs using the existing pairwise-edge factor eliminator. Tests exhaust all 16 triple systems on four points by direct edge-subset enumeration, include isolated points and repeated triples, and reject changed coefficients, changed histograms and invalid domains. Relabeling and the 823 decomposition provide further controls. The coefficient sum 2^32 matches the connected graph's cycle-space dimension 66-35+1=32; it is a checksum, not a standalone proof of each coefficient. These are separate algorithms written/run by the same author, not independent human review or Lean verification.

The scalar histogram A is sufficient because the point signs are uniform and the two diagonal weights agree. Unequal class masses need at least joint information about A and label population; unequal diagonal entries need richer local face-type counts. The polynomial must not be transferred to those domains without a new identity. O-M073-01 remains open, and O-M073-02 remains open pending independent proof/scope review.

The reusable research method is: specify one invariant, give exact maps between representations, carry normalization and assumptions through every map, derive a consequence that was not recorded, and check it through a different representation. Symbol alignment exposes previously uncomputed consequences of the input; it does not supply new empirical information or establish priority by itself.
