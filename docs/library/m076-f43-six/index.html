M076 — sharp six-deletion stability of the 54-set sunflower family

Date: 2026-11-02 UTC. Claim: #203. Work ID: X-M076-F43-SIX. Scope SHA-256: 4f496055dfe5e04510c70e69ad478b502c4cb25fb17c5c4ae9fc591d1c70ab09.

Classification: author-derived, exact, restricted theorem candidate, with two algorithmically distinct finite cover enumerators and an independently represented intersection checker. No external mathematical or novelty review has occurred. This does not improve the global sunflower bound, settle the Erdős–Rado conjecture, or independently validate the earlier five-deletion lemma.

Precisely quantified statement#

Let (F) be the classical 54-member family of four-element sets on (U=\{0,\ldots,17\}): partition (U) into two disjoint nine-point components, each with three disjoint blocks of three points, and in each component take every four-set containing two points from each of two distinct blocks. A three-petal sunflower consists of three distinct sets whose three pairwise intersections are equal.

Six-deletion theorem candidate. For every ambient ground set (V\supseteq U), every (D\subseteq F) with (|D|\le6), and every collection (I\subseteq\binom V4\setminus F) of distinct new four-sets, if ((F\setminus D)\cup I) is sunflower-free, then (|I|\le2). This is sharp at (|D|=6). More precisely, at exactly six deletions, up to the automorphisms of (F), any admissible new set has one of only two shapes: a four-set entirely in (U) with support block counts ((2,1,1)), or (T\cup\{x\}) for one outside point (x\notin U) and a triple (T) with counts ((2,1,0)). For each six-deletion pattern that permits any new set, exactly one inside four-set or one outside triple (T) is admissible.

In the latter case, two distinct outside completions are possible only in the orbit of the deletion set (\{0,1,2,24,25,26\}) (indices in base_family()), with (T=\{0,1,3\}). The sharp example deletes these six old sets and inserts (\{0,1,3,18\}) and (\{0,1,3,19\}); the resulting family has 50 sets and passes a literal check of all (\binom{50}{3}=19,600) triples. Three distinct completions of (T) form a sunflower, so two is optimal. The stronger conjecture “at most one insertion after six deletions” is false.

This extends the existing five-deletion rigidity candidate by one deletion step. In particular, there is no 55-set construction obtained from this (F) by deleting at most six sets. No conclusion follows for seven or more deletions, or for unrelated sunflower-free families.

Finite certificate and proof#

  1. Automorphism reduction, including arbitrarily many outsiders. The permutations of points inside each three-point block, permutations of the three blocks inside each nine-point component, exchange of the two components, and arbitrary permutations of outside points preserve (F). For a candidate four-set (C\notin F), its orbit is determined by the number of outside points (0–4) and the unordered pair of descending three-tuples of its occupancy counts in the three blocks of each component. There are exactly 18 possible types (8, 5, 3, 1, 1 by outside count). All appear among the (\binom{22}{4}-54=7,261) nonmembers on a 22-point support. Thus the finite representative enumeration is exhaustive for any ambient (V); outside labels are interchangeable but not assumed absent.
  1. Forbidden-pair graph. For each representative (C), form a graph (H_C) on the 54 old members, with edge (ij) iff (F_i,F_j,C) have equal pairwise intersections. Any deletion set permitting (C) must cover every edge of (H_C). The deterministic greedy edge scan in scripts/m076_f43_six.py constructs seven vertex-disjoint edges for 16 of the 18 types, ruling out every cover of size six. The two exceptions are the inside ((2,1,1)) type (six disjoint edges) and the one-outsider ((2,1,0)) type (five). Every edge and its vertex-disjointness are verified from literal set intersections.
  1. Complete six-cover enumeration, two ways. In the inside case, a six-cover must select exactly one endpoint of each of six disjoint edges; enumerate at most (2^6=64) possibilities and test all forbidden edges. Exactly 32 are covers. In the outside case, choose one endpoint of each of five disjoint edges and a sixth arbitrary old-set index; enumerate at most (2^5\cdot49=1,568) possibilities (duplicates removed), yielding 97 six-covers. A separate recursive branch on an uncovered edge, with a safe disjoint-matching lower-bound prune, yields the identical 32 and 97 sets. This covers all six-deletion patterns that admit any candidate after conjugating by an automorphism.
  1. All potentially addable shapes. By step 2, no candidate from any other orbit can be inserted, regardless of which six old sets were removed. Enumerate the 162 inside candidates of the surviving type and the 108 old-support triples of the outside type, using one canonical outside label. For each of the 32+97 six-covers, test the old-old forbidden-pair graph of all 270 candidates. Every inside cover admits exactly its representative inside four-set and no outside triple; every outside cover admits exactly its representative old triple and no inside four-set. These 129 exhaustive finite checks are independently replayed with frozenset intersections instead of integer bit masks. An additional orbit-key-free, direct 22-point frozenset sweep examines all 7,261 alternative four-sets and finds exactly 594 candidates not excluded by a seven-edge greedy matching: 162 inside candidates and 432 outside completions (108 triples times four possible outsider labels). Normalizing the latter labels gives exactly the 270-candidate catalog. This challenges the orbit classification by an independent direct enumeration.
  1. Simultaneous insertion. An inside cover admits only one new four-set. For the 97 outside covers, 96 retain exactly one old set containing the admissible triple (T); it forms a sunflower with any two distinct sets (T\cup\{x\},T\cup\{y\}), so at most one insertion is possible. Exactly one six-cover retains zero old sets containing (T): (\{0,1,2,24,25,26\}), and two completions with distinct outsiders pass the literal all-triples checker. Any three completions form a sunflower with core (T). Therefore (|I|\le2) for exactly six deletions. For fewer deletions, extend (D) arbitrarily to a six-set; admissibility only increases, so the same upper bound holds.

The proof is an exhaustive finite certificate with an explicit completeness argument. It does not rely on a SAT solver verdict, heuristic search, randomized sampling or an unproved classification of all 54-member sunflower-free families.

The first command checks 18 orbit types, 16 seven-edge exclusions, two independently implemented cover enumerators, the 129 cover classifications, a separate frozenset-based reconstruction of all forbidden graphs, the full 7,261-candidate direct sweep, and the sharpness and three-insertion negative controls. Tests reject a forged deletion cover and a purported pair of insertions under each of the 96 nonexceptional outside covers; they check the inside-only case and replay all 270 candidate forbidden graphs with two set representations.

Observed exact counts: 18 types; 16 excluded; 32 inside six-covers; 97 outside six-covers; 162 inside candidate sets and 108 outside support triples; 96 outside covers with one surviving old superset of the triple and one exceptional cover with none; maximal simultaneous insertions 2. All counts concern representatives or their cover patterns, not distinct labeled six-deletion sets globally.

Literature boundary, failed avenues, and next falsifier#

  • Abbott, Hanson and Sauer, Intersection Theorems for Systems of Sets, JCTA 12 (1972), 381–389, DOI: historical construction context.
  • Abbott and Hanson, On finite Δ-systems, Discrete Mathematics 8 (1974), 1–12, DOI; and On finite Δ-systems II (1977), DOI: older finite Δ-system literature to audit for implicit local-stability results.
  • Axante, Budala, Chitic, Dumitru and Nacu, Sunflower-Free Uniform Families: Recursive Constructions and Explicit Bounds, arXiv:2609.06175 (posted September 5, 2026): gives (54\le f(4,3)\le83), not this exact six-deletion theorem.
  • Previous five-deletion result, with its pinned OEIS witness and original literature notes.

Failed stronger route: the natural guess “six deletions still permit at most one insertion” is refuted by the explicit 50-member two-insertion example. The naive idea that a six-edge matching alone excludes a six-cover is also false: the 32 inside covers show equality is possible. Directly enumerating all (\binom{54}{6}) deletion patterns is unnecessary; the matching-product reduction and branch-on-edge replay give a complete small certificate.

Limits: The two algorithmic paths and representation replay were produced by the same author; they are not independent mathematical or historical-novelty review. A targeted literature search did not find the precise six-deletion bound, which does not establish historical priority. The next falsifier is an independently written orbit/action checker and an external audit of the 1972–1992 Δ-system construction literature. A seven-deletion configuration admitting at least eight new sets would be the first local route to a 55-set improvement, but this result says nothing about whether it exists. No registry evidence or solved status changes are warranted.
