M097 — a direct hub edge in a multitheta graphic metric

Date: 2026-11-02 UTC. Claim: #228. Work ID: X-M097-CHORDED-MULTITHETA. Scope SHA-256: 66124a539f83fe23968a5896b795c2361a3a8ddc8acd1da4afce68093819c5ab.

Status: Author-derived all-orders restricted theorem candidate, exact finite checks and a separate LP implementation. Historical novelty and independent specialist acceptance not established. This is not a new global bound for metric TSP, and does not settle the 4/3 conjecture. The unchorded multitheta proof candidate is M097_MULTITHETA.md; the chord case was deliberately excluded there.

Exact statement#

Let k>=2 and L_1,...,L_k>=2 be integers. Form a finite simple unweighted graph G with two hubs u,v, a direct edge uv, and k internally vertex-disjoint u-v paths of lengths L_i, with no other edges. Write m=sum_i L_i and ell=min_i L_i. On the complete-graph shortest-path metric c_G, use the symmetric subtour LP with nonnegative edge variables, degree exactly two, and cut weight at least two for every nontrivial cut.

Restricted theorem candidate:

SEP(c_G) = m
TSP(c_G) = m + (k mod 2) * min(1, ell - 2).

Thus the subtour LP is unchanged by the added direct hub edge, despite the metric becoming shorter. If k is even, or any long path has length two, the integrality gap is one. Otherwise k is odd and ell>=3, and the gap is 1+1/m <= 10/9, with equality at k=3, L=(3,3,3). This bound is specific to the chorded class.

Proof: fractional lower bound and explicit primal#

Take any feasible subtour point x on the complete metric. For every pair a,b, route x_ab along a shortest a-b path in G and call the resulting original-edge load y. Routing preserves cost, and y(delta(S))>=x(delta(S))>=2 for every cut.

On any long constituent path P with L>=2 edges e_1,...,e_L, the internal segment strictly between any two distinct edges e_i,e_j has graph boundary exactly those two edges: all internal path vertices have degree two, and the added hub edge never touches them. Consequently y(e_i)+y(e_j)>=2. Summing the L(L-1)/2 inequalities gives sum_{e in P}y_e>=L. Sum over the k long paths; the hub-edge load is nonnegative. Therefore SEP(c_G)>=m.

For a matching primal, let p_i and q_i be the first and last internal vertices of path i. Put alpha=2/k on each long-path edge incident to a hub, weight one on each remaining long-path edge, and gamma=(k-2)/(k(k-1)) on each shortcut p_i p_j and each q_i q_j for i<j. Coincident shortcut contributions (length-two paths) are added, not overwritten. Put zero on all other complete-graph edges, including uv.

Every vertex has degree two: hubs have k alpha=2, and each internal vertex receives 2 alpha+2(k-1)gamma=2 if it is both first and last, or 1+alpha+(k-1)gamma=2 otherwise. To verify all subtour cuts, for each hub count t adjacent internal vertices on the opposite side. Its spoke-plus-shortcut contribution is F(t)=alpha*t+gamma*t*(k-t), with F(0)=0, F(1)=1, F(k)=2, and F(t)>=1 for 1<=t<=k (concavity, including k=2). If both hubs have positive counts, the cut is at least two. If exactly one count is positive and equals k, that hub alone contributes two. If exactly one is strictly between zero and k, the endpoint membership on a suitable long path forces at least one interior unit-edge crossing, making the cut at least two. If both counts vanish and the hubs lie on opposite sides, each long path forces an interior unit-edge crossing (at least k>=2); if the hubs lie on the same side, a nontrivial cut must isolate an internal segment and has at least two interior unit-edge crossings. These cases include length-two paths (where the first and last internal vertices coincide).

Every used long-path edge costs one in the chorded metric; every used same-hub shortcut between different paths costs exactly two. The primal cost is m-2*k+2*k*alpha + 2*k*(k-1)*gamma = m. Thus SEP(c_G)=m. The proof also follows by observing that the unchorded feasible point has cost m and adding the hub edge cannot increase its metric cost; the explicit cut argument above avoids relying on an independently unreviewed earlier theorem.

Proof: integral tour parity#

A complete-metric Hamiltonian tour expands to a connected spanning Eulerian edge-multigraph on G with the same cost; conversely an Eulerian spanning walk shortcuts to a metric Hamiltonian tour without increasing cost. On every long path, the two edge multiplicities at an internal degree-two vertex have the same parity, so all edges of a given path have the same parity.

An odd-mode path costs at least L_i. An even-mode path has at most one edge of multiplicity zero (otherwise an intervening internal vertex segment is disconnected); all other edges have multiplicity at least two. It costs at least 2(L_i-1)=L_i+(L_i-2). If q long paths are odd-mode and the direct edge has multiplicity t, even degree at a hub requires t congruent q (mod 2).

For even k, all long paths once and no direct edge form a connected Eulerian spanning graph of cost m, which meets the pathwise lower bound. For odd k, all long paths once plus the direct edge form an Eulerian spanning graph of cost m+1. If ell=2, a better graph of cost m uses every long path except a length-two path once, and on that short path doubles one edge and omits the other (the internal vertex remains attached); the direct edge is omitted. If ell>=3, every even-mode path costs at least one above its baseline, while all-odd mode requires the direct edge an odd number of times, so every tour costs at least m+1. This establishes the formula.

Optimal-face rigidity, support theorem, and quantitative stability (2026-11-02)#

Author-derived restricted lemmas (same claim #228). Let (x) be any feasible symmetric subtour-LP point on the complete shortest-path metric, with objective (m+\varepsilon), where (\varepsilon\ge0). Let (B) be the set of unordered vertex pairs (\{a,b\}) for which at least one shortest route in the original graph uses the direct hub edge (uv). Then

[ \sum_{\{a,b\}\in B}x_{ab}\le\varepsilon. ]

Consequently, every optimum vanishes on every pair in (B), not just on (uv). For every long path of length (L\ge3), every shortest-route load (y_e) satisfies

[ 1-\frac{\varepsilon}{L-2}\le y_e\le1+\varepsilon. ]

In particular, at an optimum all loads on each path of length at least three equal one, regardless of shortest-route tie-breaking. A length-two path is exceptional: its two loads sum to two, but they need not separately equal one.

Stronger quantitative sparse-support theorem. Define (H) as the set of original long-path edges together with all same-hub shortcuts joining the first internal vertices of different paths or the last internal vertices of different paths. Then for every feasible subtour point, not only optimal points,

[ \boxed{\sum_{\{a,b\}\notin H}x_{ab}\le\varepsilon.} ]

The coefficient one is sharp, including for the three-path length-three example below. In particular, the entire optimal face is supported on (H). This strictly strengthens the chord-geodesic inequality: for (k=3), lengths ((4,4,4)), there are 24 metric pairs outside (H) which are not in the chord-geodesic set (B).

Tight-cut theorem. At every subtour optimum, each cut separating a nonempty contiguous segment of internal vertices of any constituent long path from the remaining graph is tight: (x(\delta(S))=2). This includes the (\binom{L}{2}) interval cuts specified by two distinct boundary edges of a length-(L) path. Moreover, every positive variable (x_{ab}) is supported on one of the following pairs: (i) an original long-path edge; (ii) the two first internal vertices of different long paths, adjacent to (u); or (iii) the two last internal vertices of different long paths, adjacent to (v). In particular, no other metric edge can belong to the optimal face, even if its shortest route avoids the hub chord. For length-two paths the first and last internal vertices coincide, and coincident shortcut types are understood as one unordered pair. The support has exactly (m+k(k-1)-\binom{t}{2}) pairs, where (t) is the number of length-two paths (the only duplication occurs when both endpoints of a pair of paths coincide). This is a support necessity, not a claim that every feasible assignment on the displayed support is optimal.

For (k=3) and lengths ((4,4,4)), this rules out 24 additional metric pairs beyond the 13 chord-geodesic pairs: only 18 of the 55 complete-graph edges can support a subtour optimum. These counts are exact and independently replayable, not a global graph-metric theorem.

Proof of quantitative bounds. Route every positive metric edge along an arbitrary shortest path in the original graph, obtaining nonnegative loads (y) with total load (m+\varepsilon). Every original cut has load at least two: routing can only increase the load of a cut. For any two distinct edges (e,f) on the same long path, the internal segment between them has boundary exactly (\{e,f\}), hence (y_e+y_f\ge2). Summing over pairs gives (\sum_{e\in P}y_e\ge L). Write (\delta_P=\sum_{e\in P}y_e-L\ge0). Because the original graph consists of the long paths and (uv),

[ \varepsilon=y_{uv}+\sum_P\delta_P. ]

Choose a shortest route through (uv) for every pair in (B); routes for different pairs can be selected independently. Then (y_{uv}\ge\sum_{ab\in B}x_{ab}), proving the first inequality.

For a path of length (L\ge3), put (z_e=y_e-1). The pair inequalities give (z_e+z_f\ge0). If (z_e=-t<0), every other (z_f\ge t), whence (\delta_P\ge(L-2)t). If (z_e=t>0), at most one other deviation is negative, and the remaining deviations compensate it, so (\delta_P\ge t). As (0\le\delta_P\le\varepsilon), both bounds follow.

Proof of the quantitative sparse-support inequality. For each internal vertex (w), let (\tau_w) be the total (x)-mass of metric pairs whose chosen shortest route passes through (w) as an interior vertex. Every routed path is simple, so

[ 2\tau_w=y(\delta(\{w\}))-x(\delta(\{w\})). ]

For a constituent path (P) with edge loads (y_1,\ldots,y_L), put (T_P=\sum_{w\in P^\circ}\tau_w). Summing the singleton identities and using the LP degree equations gives the exact identity

[ 2T_P=2\delta_P-(y_1+y_L-2). ]

The segment cut between the first and last edges gives (y_1+y_L\ge2), even when (L=2). Thus (T_P\le\delta_P).

Every metric pair outside (H), on any shortest route, must either traverse the chord (uv) or pass through at least one internal path vertex other than its endpoints: otherwise its route has no interior vertices except possibly a single hub and is precisely one of the pairs defining (H). Counting such a pair against the chord load or one of the transit masses (and allowing overcounting) gives

[ \sum_{ab\notin H}x_{ab} \le y_{uv}+\sum_{w\text{ internal}}\tau_w = y_{uv}+\sum_P T_P \le y_{uv}+\sum_P\delta_P =\varepsilon. ]

The argument works for all path lengths at least two, any number of paths (k\ge2), and arbitrary shortest-path tie-breaking. It requires the degree equations as well as the subtour inequalities. At (\varepsilon=0), all mass outside (H) vanishes.

Proof of tight cuts and support. At an optimum, (\varepsilon=0). Every length-(L\ge3) path therefore has (y_e=1) on each edge. For (L=2), its two edge loads sum to two. Thus for every contiguous nonempty segment (S) of internal vertices, its boundary consists of two path edges with total load (y(\delta(S))=2). Since (2\le x(\delta(S))\le y(\delta(S))), the cut is tight.

In particular, for each internal vertex (w), the singleton cut is tight and (y(\delta(\{w\}))=x(\delta(\{w\}))=2). For any choice of shortest routes, a routed metric edge with (w) as an internal vertex contributes two to the routed singleton-cut load but zero to the original metric singleton-cut load. Hence

[ y(\delta(\{w\}))-x(\delta(\{w\})) =2\sum_{\substack{\{a,b\}:\w\text{ internal to chosen }a-b\text{ route}}}x_{ab}=0. ]

Every term is nonnegative. Because shortest routes can be selected independently and arbitrarily, no positive metric pair can admit any shortest route through an internal path vertex other than its endpoints. The chord-geodesic inequality also excludes any pair admitting a shortest route through (uv). A shortest route between distinct endpoints whose interior contains neither an internal path vertex nor (uv) must therefore be a single original long-path edge, or a two-edge route via one hub. The latter connects first internal vertices on different paths or last internal vertices on different paths. This proves the support claim.

Sharp coefficient and integral boundary. For three paths (0-2-3-1), (0-4-5-1), (0-6-7-1), the metric Hamiltonian cycle (0-1-3-2-4-5-7-6-0) has cost ten, exactly (m+1). Its only metric edge in (B) is (\{0,1\}), so (\sum_{ab\in B}x_{ab}=1=\varepsilon). Thus coefficient one is sharp. This optimal integral tour uses the chord, although the fractional optimum (value nine) cannot use it.

Adversarial checks and falsifiers. The separately represented checker in scripts/m097_chorded_optimal_face.py builds shortest routes, checks the chord-geodesic set and all subtour cuts, and tests exact optimal and perturbed feasible points. The new support/tight-cut audit checks the quantitative outside-support mass bound, tests all (\binom{L}{2}) interval cuts, all singleton transit loads, and every positive metric pair against the predicted sparse support. It also uses exhaustive simple-path DFS to challenge the shortest-path classification, and independent Hamiltonian-permutation sweeps to attack the coefficient-one bound on all tours of selected small graphs. In total, 10,950 complete Hamiltonian tours across six small instances were checked against the outside-support mass bound, with no violations and many nonzero equality cases; this is a finite adversarial check, not the all-orders proof. The initially stronger claim that every length-two instance has multiple chord-geodesic pairs was falsified: for lengths (2,2) and (2,2,2), only (\{u,v\}) qualifies. The test was corrected; the mathematical lemmas were unaffected.

A falsifier would be a feasible rational point of cost (m+\varepsilon) with outside-support mass exceeding (\varepsilon), a non-tight internal interval cut at optimum, or a violation of either pathwise stability inequality. An external reviewer should audit cut-routing, shortest-path tie cases, the equality step for singleton cuts, and prior literature. Same-author code paths are not independent specialist mathematical acceptance.

Observed author-side exact sweep: 92 nondecreasing tuples, 2<=k<=5, 2<=L_i<=7, and at most 12 vertices; 45 even-k, 35 odd-k with a length-two path, 12 odd-k with all lengths at least three. Each instance reconstructs the shortest-path metric by BFS, verifies the proposed rational LP point against every degree and nontrivial cut, and independently computes the exact Hamiltonian tour optimum by subset DP (not by the parity formula). Seven targeted adversarial tests pass, including altered LP degree, invalid parameters, the direct edge's presence, parity boundaries and a false naive extension.

A separate, optional SciPy HiGHS implementation, written with a different graph representation and all full-subtour inequalities, independently optimized 20 instances of at most eight vertices and matched SEP=m. This is author-side cross-checking, not an independent specialist review.

Additional adversarial min-cut replay (same author, separate algorithm). The standalone scripts/m097_chorded_mincut.py reconstructs the rational witness from scratch as an integer-capacity weighted graph scaled by k(k-1), without importing the original checker. It checks degree 2k(k-1) at every vertex, cost m k(k-1), and the global cut minimum 2k(k-1) via a separately implemented Stoer–Wagner algorithm. The cut algorithm is itself compared with brute-force bipartitions on 210 seeded arbitrary weighted graphs with 2–8 vertices, including disconnected cases. The seven large/boundary examples include 20 length-two paths (22 vertices, cut 760 at scale 380), 13 length-three paths (28 vertices, cut 312 at scale 156), and four lengths (5,7,9,11) (30 vertices, cut 24 at scale 12). A one-unit edge-capacity tamper reduces a singleton cut below the bound, and invalid-length inputs are rejected. The independent algorithm and representation reduce shared implementation risk, but are still author-produced checks, not external mathematical review. These checks do not establish historical novelty or the completeness of the paper search.

Failed extrapolations and boundaries: (1) Treating the direct edge as a length-one path in the old unchorded formula predicts SEP=m+1, which is false already for k=2,L=(2,2) (actual SEP=4, not 5). (2) A direct hub edge does not always eliminate the fractional gap: k=3,L=(3,3,3) has SEP=9,TSP=10. (3) The restriction k>=2 matters: with only one long path, G is a simple cycle and SEP=TSP=L+1, not L. (4) Adding the hub edge does not necessarily shorten the optimal tour; for even k, both chorded and unchorded formulas equal m. (5) The first tempting extension, allowing one cross-path edge, already falsifies SEP=m: start with k=3, lengths (3,3,3) and paths 0-2-3-1, 0-4-5-1, 0-6-7-1, plus hub edge 0-1; add only cross edge 2-5. The 8-edge Hamiltonian cycle 0-4-5-2-3-1-7-6-0 proves TSP<=8. Every metric edge has cost at least one and the degree-two equations imply SEP>=8, hence SEP=TSP=8<m=9. This is an exact literal counterexample to extending the LP formula beyond degree-two path interiors, not a counterexample to the restricted theorem or to the 4/3 conjecture. An independent exploratory SciPy full-cut LP and subset tour DP gave the same values; the displayed cycle and trivial lower bound are the rigorous certificate.

Primary literature, limits and next falsifiers#

  • Held and Karp, The Traveling-Salesman Problem and Minimum Spanning Trees, Operations Research 18 (1970), DOI: classical LP baseline.
  • Sebő and Vygen, Shorter Tours by Nicer Ears, arXiv:1201.1870 (2012): graphic TSP and 2-edge-connectivity context.
  • Qian, Schalekamp, Williamson and van Zuylen, On the Integrality Gap of the Subtour LP for the 1,2-TSP, Mathematical Programming 150 (2015), author page: distinct metric subclass.
  • Boyd, Sitters, van der Ster and Stougie, The Traveling Salesman Problem on Cubic and Subcubic Graphs, Mathematical Programming 144 (2014), DOI: proves a 4/3 bound for the subcubic graphic class; the present graph has hub degree k+1 and for k>=3 is not subcubic. This paper gives context, not priority for the exact formula.
  • Mnich and Mömke, Improved Integrality Gap Upper Bounds for Traveling Salesperson Problems with Distances One and Two (2018), institutional record: a 10/9 result for a particular 1,2-TSP LP-support class; equality of the numerical constant does not identify the same metric family.
  • Cook, Hougardy and Petrich, Extending Exact Integrality Gap Computations for the Metric TSP, arXiv:2603.12995 (March 2026): current small-dimensional baseline.
  • M097_MULTITHETA.md and M097_BLOCK_SUM.md: repository-local antecedents, not external priority evidence.
