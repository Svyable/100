Next 100 — hypothesis reservoir

Explore on Pages: Next 100 · Active 100

Next 100 is the intake layer for the active 100, not another claim that 120 (or eventually thousands of) new mathematical theorems are open. It holds hypothetical statements with potentially consequential spillovers across multiple fields of mathematics, scientific practice or the real world. Some are research sketches awaiting exact formalization. Some may be already proved, false, equivalent to known results, or impossible under their stated assumptions. Do not present them as discoveries.

The first release seeded 120 editorial hypotheses across 15 domains. The second controlled intake adds 96 distinct hypotheses across 12 new domains (216 total; 27 domains). Changes are submitted as reviewable cohort files. The format and statically paginated browser can grow to thousands without changing the 100-slot active list. Favor quality over numerical inflation.

Data architecture#

  • data/next_candidates.json: seed cohort with immutable N0001 IDs.
  • data/next_batches/*.json: optional additional arrays of candidates. Use a fresh batch per intake round so review diffs remain manageable.
  • data/next_review_queue.json: a deliberately small cross-domain prior-art queue with bounded first tests and context-only, not open-status, sources.
  • data/next_promotions.json: immutable provenance records mapping an N candidate into the active M slot it eventually replaces.
  • scripts/next.py: schema, duplicate, promotion and active-cap validator; deterministic discovery-priority ranking and static Pages builder.
  • docs/data/next.json: generated client snapshot, not a source of truth.
  • docs/next/: searchable Pages explorer with filters, linkable IDs and expandable motivations / first attacks.

Run python3 scripts/next.py after source edits. Run python3 scripts/next.py --check or python3 scripts/next.py --top 20. CI checks the generated artifact and browser JavaScript syntax. The --similarity 20 option reports closest lexical pairs for human triage; CI rejects highly overlapping titles or statements. Lexical distance cannot establish mathematical novelty or independence.

Candidate schema and editorial scores#

Each record has an immutable id (N followed by at least four digits), a short title, a hypothetical statement, its mathematical domain, at least two concrete spillovers, a clear consequence conditional on success, a falsifiable first_attack, a stage, evidence_state and scores. The evidence state must remain unvetted_hypothesis at intake.

  • formalize: substantial definitions, quantifiers or assumptions still needed.
  • prior_art_review: more concrete statement but literature/status unverified.

Four 1–5 editorial scores mirror the active ranking weights: impact 30%, tractability 30%, independent verifiability 25%, cross-domain transfer 15%. The discovery index = 6×impact + 6×tractability + 5×verifiability + 3×transfer. It is not a probability of success, novelty, truth or scientific validity. Ties are broken by stable ID. Promotions do not rewrite historical hypotheses.

Cohort-02 intake and review discipline#

  • 96 proposals in data/next_batches/2026-10-cohort-02.json cover information theory, codes, space, ocean, water, agriculture, transportation, geophysics, computational chemistry, measurement, institutional choice, and education. They diversify mathematical mechanisms and downstream applications.
  • The 12-item queue is not a completed status audit. Its institutional links are background starting points, not endorsements of the conjectures. Each item names a research question, a minimum toy experiment, and a literature gap.
  • scripts/next.py --similarity 20 exposes lexical overlap for review. A matching word set may indicate unrelated problems; semantically equivalent hypotheses can still evade lexical screening. Human review remains necessary.
  • Intake batches should generally contain 50–100 genuinely differentiated proposals. Hold back redundant ideas and items without two credible spillovers.

Intake → active 100#

  1. Specify. Define every term, quantifier, exception and objective. State a mathematical theorem/counterexample target, rather than an untestable statement about future technology. Describe at least two nontrivial ways resolving it would matter.
  2. Audit. Compare current specialist/primary sources and existing conjectures. Record dated URLs, exact equivalences and prior counterexamples. A missing source is not evidence that a problem is open.
  3. Attack. Identify a restricted lemma, explicit falsifier, finite instance generator or independent verifier. Add a reproducible campaign if useful.
  4. Select. Compare against the weakest/solved/duplicated active slot using both long-term significance and runnable proof-work readiness. No automatic displacement from a high discovery index alone.
  5. Promote via a reviewable GitHub PR. Replace one active record, maintain exactly 100 distinct M IDs, record the departing candidate's history in the PR, and add a promotion entry with next_id, active_id, promoted_on, review_url, and a substantive reason. The active problem must follow the full research protocol (including evidence-state rules). Promoting does not certify a proof.
  6. Check. Rebuild RANKED.md, ATTACK_QUEUE.md, lab/research and Pages snapshots with existing scripts. Run the full test suite and CI.

Never reuse an N ID for another statement. A changed or withdrawn idea should receive a documented decision, not vanish from the ledger. Retain the source even when it has been promoted. The Next 100 page preserves a direct link to the active M record once a promotion is logged.

Adding a batch#

Put an array of objects following the existing schema at, for example, data/next_batches/2026-11-round-01.json, with fresh N IDs. After literature triage and duplicate checks, run:

python3 scripts/next.py
python3 scripts/next.py --check
node --check docs/next/app.js
python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -v

This system is a reservoir of proposals, not autonomous paper monitoring or an automatic conjecture generator. Verification and promotion need documented research decisions.
