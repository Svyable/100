RESEARCH DOSSIERS

M094 — n=29 multiplier-symmetry no-go (bounded negative result)

2026-11-01 · O-M094-01 · claim #116

This is a necessary-condition obstruction to two over-restrictive search strategies, not nonexistence of Williamson matrices of order 29, Hadamard matrices of order 116, or the solution of any open Hadamard problem. It is a direct elementary consequence of the standard Williamson row-sum identity; no novelty or independent review is claimed.

Exact model and result#

Let A, B, C, D be symmetric circulant matrices of order 29 with entries in {+1,-1} and A²+B²+C²+D²=116 I. Write a, b, c, d for their first rows. For each row x, let R_x be its sum. Summing the 29 cyclic correlation equations over all shifts gives the necessary identity

R_a² + R_b² + R_c² + R_d² = 116. (1)

The zero shift contributes 4*29=116, and the other shifts contribute zero. This argument uses integer arithmetic, not floating-point spectral tests.

A common multiplier g means each of the four rows separately satisfies x_i=x_(gi mod 29) for every i. Symmetric circulants also have x_i=x_(-i). The multiplicative group F_29^ is cyclic of order 28.

  • g=12 (order 4, containing -1): the index orbits under multiplication by 12 and reversal have sizes 1,4,4,4,4,4,4,4. A row sum is e+4(2k-7), e in {±1}, 0<=k<=7. If its square is <=116, its absolute value is 3 or 5. Four such squares sum to at most 4*25=100, contradicting (1).
  • g=7 (order 7; with reversal, order 14): the index orbits have sizes 1,14,14. The row sum is e+14u+14v with e,u,v in {±1}. Under the same square bound it must be ±1. Four squares then sum to 4, contradicting (1).

Every subgroup of F_29^* properly containing {-1,+1} has order 4, 14, or 28, and therefore contains the subgroup of order 4 or 14. Thus no Williamson quadruple at n=29 can have a common multiplier symmetry strictly stronger than reversal. This is not a prohibition on using multiplier equivalence to quotient candidates, nor on different multipliers for different rows. It only excludes the extra invariance hypothesis.

Reproduction and independent-path controls#

python3 scripts/williamson_multiplier.py --check python3 scripts/williamson_multiplier.py python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -p 'test_williamson_multiplier.py' -v

The executable computes orbits by closure under two index actions and enumerates their row sums; a separate arithmetic path uses only subgroup orders and orbit lengths. Tests independently expand all 256 assignments for g=12 and eight for g=7 into full 29-sign rows, check invariance, and recount sums. Invalid multipliers and altered orbit data are rejected or detected. The reversal-only g=28 control passes the row-sum screen, which confirms the screen is not spuriously declaring all n=29 searches impossible. Passing this necessary condition is not a construction.

Negative outcome: the proposed shortcut of forcing a shared nontrivial multiplier symmetry cannot produce the missing order-116 certificate. No full 2^14 symmetric-sequence search, 116x116 orthogonality replay, order-156 or order-172 Goethals–Seidel replay, or independent reviewer assessment was performed. O-M094-01 remains open.

Next falsifier: produce a fully specified n=29 Williamson quadruple without the extra common multiplier invariance, check all 28 nonzero autocorrelation shifts by an independent path, and verify the assembled 116x116 matrix by integer dot products. Alternatively import published Goethals–Seidel base blocks for 156 or 172 with independent matrix verification. Do not infer nonexistence from a restricted symmetry search.
