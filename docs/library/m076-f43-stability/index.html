M076 — sharp local deletion barrier around the 54-set sunflower construction

Date: 2026-11-01 UTC. Claim: #185. Work id: M076-F43-SEARCH, scope SHA-256 5237be0b42bf54171a3f31bd8bc90f7bad55dd7898469fe2912e2a348267c1b4. Status: author-derived exact restricted stability lemma, independently recomputed in code but not independently mathematically reviewed; historical novelty unestablished. No new global lower bound and no solution of the Erdős–Rado sunflower conjecture.

Exact statement and result#

A three-petal sunflower is a triple of distinct sets A,B,C with A∩B=A∩C=B∩C, including empty core. Let F be the classical 54-member family on 18 points built from two disjoint nine-point components. In each component, partition the nine points into three blocks of three; take every four-set formed by choosing two points from each of two distinct blocks. Each component contributes 3 × 3 × 3 = 27 sets.

Restricted local stability lemma (finite, sharp). For any four-element set C not in F, on any ambient ground set, if (F∖D)∪{C} contains no three-petal sunflower, then |D|≥5. If C is contained in the original 18-point support, then |D|≥6. Both inequalities are attained. Stronger five-deletion rigidity: for every D⊆F with |D|≤5, at most one new four-set can be adjoined to F∖D while preserving sunflower-freeness. For |D|≤4, no new four-set can be adjoined.

This is an obstruction to replacing a small number of sets in this particular construction, not an upper bound on all sunflower-free families. In particular, no improvement from 54 to 55 can be obtained by deleting at most five members of this F and inserting arbitrary new four-sets. With exactly five deletions, the resulting family has at most 50 sets (and this bound is attained). The search at deletion distance six and beyond is open.

Proof and exact certificate#

  1. The 54 old sets contain no three-petal sunflower. All C(54,3)=24,804 triples pass the direct equality test. The source construction is OEIS A332077, Abbott–Hanson–Sauer witness; the ordered 1-based brace-list generated by the script, with a final LF, has SHA-256 b84ccc5d91526d89d372e19de5025bc2ff63994c615390a715516831c391a646.
  2. For a candidate C, form a graph H_C whose vertices are the 54 members of F, with an edge AB precisely when A,B,C are a sunflower. Any deletion set D allowing C must be a vertex cover of H_C. Therefore a matching of t disjoint edges in H_C forces |D|≥t.
  3. Permuting points within each three-point block, permuting the three blocks within each nine-point component, swapping the two components, and relabeling all outside points preserves F. Any four-set has at most four outside points. Its orbit is therefore determined by the number r=0,1,2,3,4 of outsiders and the unordered pair of descending triples of counts in the blocks of each component. Excluding C∈F leaves exactly 18 orbit types: 8, 5, 3, 1, 1 for r=0,1,2,3,4, respectively.
  4. scripts/m076_f43_stability.py contains a literal list of six pairwise vertex-disjoint edges of H_C for 17 of the 18 orbit types. The sole exception is r=1 with old-support block counts (2,1,0) in one component and (0,0,0) in the other; it has a five-edge matching. The checker validates every edge by exact bitwise set intersection and checks that no old family member appears twice. Symmetry transfers these certificates to every four-set on an arbitrary ambient universe. This proves the lower bounds and isolates the only candidate type permitted by five deletions.
  5. For the exceptional representative C={0,1,3,18}, a complete branch-on-uncovered-edge enumeration of the forbidden-pair graph finds exactly two vertex covers of size five: {0,2,24,25,26} and {1,2,24,25,26}. Under either deletion, checking all C(18,3)=816 old-support triples shows that only T={0,1,3} can be extended with an outside point. Hence every admissible new four-set is T∪{x} for some outsider x. At least one old set containing T survives under either cover; it forms a sunflower with any two distinct such new sets. Thus at most one new set can be inserted. Symmetry proves the statement for every five-deletion set admitting a candidate.
  6. Sharpness for outside points: with zero-based labels, C={0,1,3,18} can be added after deleting old-set indices {1,2,24,25,26}, leaving 50 members and no sunflower. Sharpness within the support: C={0,1,3,6} can be added after deleting indices {1,10,11,21,22,26}, leaving 49 members and no sunflower. Both are checked by literal enumeration of all remaining triples.

The proof does not depend on a SAT/LP solver, randomized search, an unverified optimization status, or a claimed theorem outside the encoded 54-set family.

Independent computational path and adversarial controls#

python3 scripts/m076_f43_stability.py --check
python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -p 'test_m076_f43_stability.py' -v
python3 scripts/regen.py --check
python3 -m unittest discover -s tests

The first path checks the 18 explicit orbit certificates, a complete branch-on-edge five-cover enumeration, and the unique old-support triple forced by each cover. A second exhaustive method scans all 3,162,510 five-element deletion subsets directly and confirms exactly the same two covers, without branch-on-edge recursion. A separate implementation in the same file uses Python frozenset intersections, does not use the orbit reduction or the matching table, and directly sweeps all 7,261 alternative four-sets on a 22-point universe. These cover all outsider-count patterns (0–4); the proof above explains why extra ambient points introduce no further orbit type. Sixteen adversarial tests reject a duplicated family member, an injected sunflower, a repeated matching vertex, a false sunflower edge, and an incorrect deletion witness, and confirm the two sharpness examples.

This is independent algorithmic cross-checking by the author, not an independent specialist review or an external theorem acceptance.

Primary literature, assumptions, negative results and limits#

  • Abbott, Hanson, Sauer, Intersection Theorems for Systems of Sets, JCTA 12 (1972), 381–389, DOI: historical construction and finite sunflower background.
  • Abbott and Hanson, On finite Δ-systems, Discrete Mathematics 8 (1974), 1–12, DOI: historical lower-bound context.
  • Axante, Budala, Chitic, Dumitru, Nacu, arXiv:2609.06175v1 (September 2026): 54 ≤ f(4,3) ≤ 83, with an intersecting-family bound 27.
  • Babanskyy, aiXiv:2610.01292v1 (October 2026): author-claimed improvement f(4,3) ≤ 68, with finite computations; independent specialist acceptance is not established. Neither source was taken as proof of this new local lemma.
  • Rao, The story of sunflowers, JLMS 113 (January 2026): contemporary survey of the still-open uniform Erdős–Rado conjecture. The different Erdős–Szemerédi ground-set variant must not be conflated with it.

Failed avenue: A direct one-set extension of the 54-set construction is impossible; the stronger exact certificate shows that even allowing up to four deletions cannot permit any new four-set, and five deletions allow at most one new set. This is a global result about the stated local neighborhood, not an exhaustive search of all sunflower-free families or a new 55-set construction. The matching/cover argument gives no information about simultaneous insertions after deleting six or more old sets. The 54-set construction is highly symmetric, so no generalization to other 54-member families follows. A targeted prior-art search did not locate this exact deletion-distance statement, but absence of a search hit is not proof of historical novelty.

Next falsifier: Independently implement the 18-type symmetry reduction and verify the literal edge matchings from the OEIS witness; challenge the sharpness deletion sets. Then search for a 55-member family using at least six deletions and at least seven insertions, or a completely different construction, with exact triple-checking. Audit older extremal-set-system literature for the precise deletion-distance lemma before making any novelty claim. The existing PR-landing task is separate and unchanged.
