M078 — induced P₄ separates the six-vertex degree–triangle collisions

Work packet: O-M078-01, scope digest c25d1f1ccd3a55bdd178da6dcb4747199d51a0190d9321ccb7e1b21af505fe62. Claim: #126. Classification: exact finite proposition with computational exhaustion and a double-path verifier. Independent mathematical and statement-fidelity review pending. Novelty in the wider literature has NOT been established. This does not resolve the unrestricted graph reconstruction conjecture.

Precise finite proposition#

Let G be a simple, undirected graph on exactly six vertices. Define

  • D(G): the sorted degree sequence, an isomorphism invariant readable from the deck of vertex-deleted cards;
  • T(G): the number of triangles;
  • P₄(G): the number of four-element vertex subsets inducing a path of length three, not the number of arbitrary non-induced length-three walks.

Sharp signature theorem (finite proof plus explicit obstruction): among the 156 isomorphism classes of six-vertex graphs, the signature

(D(G), T(G), P₄(G))

is injective. The weaker signature (D(G), T(G)) has exactly 20 collision classes, each containing two nonisomorphic graphs. In 14 of those pairs, the P₄ counts differ by one; in the other six, by three. No pair remains ambiguous. Conversely, for every n >= 7, there exist two nonisomorphic n-vertex graphs having the same three-coordinate signature. The positive side n <= 6 follows by census; the negative side for all n >= 7 follows from one explicit seven-vertex pair and isolate padding.

Illustrative certificate pair: edge codes 10376 and 25601 share degree sequence (1,1,1,1,2,2), with zero triangles, but have 0 and 1 induced P₄ instances respectively. These codes are not graph identifiers independent of our bit convention: bit positions represent the edge pairs (0,1), (0,2), …, (4,5), ordered lexicographically.

Explicit obstruction at seven vertices and every larger order#

Set (G = K_{1,3} \sqcup K_3), a four-vertex star disjoint from a triangle. Set (H=P_3 \sqcup W), where (P_3) is a three-vertex path and (W) is the paw: a triangle with a pendant leaf.

The two degree multisets are identical:

[ D(G)=D(H)=(1,1,1,2,2,2,3). ]

Each graph contains exactly one triangle. Neither contains an induced four-vertex path: each of the two components in each graph is too small to contribute an induced P₄ except its own four-vertex component, and neither (K_{1,3}) nor the paw is (P_4). Thus (P₄(G)=P₄(H)=0), so the signatures agree.

They are not isomorphic: the triangle in (G) constitutes an entire connected component of size 3; the triangle in (H) lies in a connected component of size 4. Equivalently, the size-4 components are a star versus a paw, which have different edge counts.

For any integer (t \ge 0), the graphs (G\sqcup tK_1) and (H\sqcup tK_1) remain nonisomorphic, while their degrees acquire the same (t) zeros and their triangle and induced-P₄ counts stay unchanged. This proves failure for every n=7+t, without requiring an exhaustive large-n search. The explicit edge lists and edge-bit codes are stored under sharpness_witness_n7 in the certificate.

Why the invariant really comes from the deck#

We use the classical Kelly double-counting identity (1957), not a new lemma. Let s_H(G) count induced vertex subsets of G whose induced graph is isomorphic to a fixed H with h vertices, where h<n=|V(G)|. Each copy of H occurs in precisely n−h vertex-deleted cards: deleting any vertex outside that copy retains it, and deleting one inside destroys it. Hence

[ (n-h)\,s_H(G) = \sum_{v\in V(G)} s_H(G-v). ]

For H=P₄ and n=6 this becomes

[ P₄(G) = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{v\in V(G)}P₄(G-v). ]

Therefore P₄(G) is determined by the unordered multiset of vertex-deleted cards; it does not require reconstructing G first. Similarly T(G) is obtained from the card triangles divided by n−3=3. The edge count is recovered from card edge totals divided by n−2=4; then the degree multiset follows by subtracting each card's edge count from the recovered edge count. Thus all three coordinates are deck-readable, rather than merely directly computable on a labelled graph.

A source for Kelly's identity and its induced-subgraph form is Dalili et al., Discrete Mathematics 308 (2008), Lemma 3.2: reconstruction.pdf. See also the discussion of induced k-decks in Groenland–Johnston–Scott–Tan, Reconstruction from smaller cards. Neither source should be read as claiming this particular six-vertex three-coordinate signature is novel.

Exhaustive proof certificate and independent verifier#

Run from the repository root:

python3 scripts/m078_p4_separator.py --check --json
python3 -m unittest tests/test_m078_p4_separator.py -v

The committed certificate is data/reference/m078_p4_pairs.json. It lists the 20 colliding pairs in lexicographic edge-bit-code order, their common degree sequence and triangle count, and both P₄ counts. A SHA-256 hash pins the canonical JSON serialization of those 20 records:

f00c172f4d827c71b6cfaf3c83f7a9b00cf6fce1c620be6a47f78b03d634120e

The checker is not a printout of a trusted search result. It independently:

  1. Constructs the complete 156-isomorphism-class census using orderly graph extension.
  2. Certifies the count by Burnside's lemma and independently validates nonisomorphism with degree-respecting canonical edge-list forms.
  3. Counts P₄ directly from induced degree patterns (1,1,2,2).
  4. Recounts P₄ from all five-vertex cards using a separate predicate: a connected three-edge tree whose maximum degree is at most two. The star K₁,₃ is an adversarial negative control; connected and three edges alone are not sufficient.
  5. Reconstructs the other signature coordinates from card edge and triangle counts, rebuilds all collision pairs from these independently computed values, and compares them with the committed certificate.
  6. Rejects mutated records and digests through automated tests.

Completeness of the finite claim follows from the census certification plus verification that every collision pair has unequal P₄ counts. It does not provide a symbolic classification of arbitrary n-vertex graphs.

Limits and useful next question#

This is an exact statement only for n=6. At n=5, degree sequence and triangle count already distinguish the 34 graph classes. Neither number, nor P₄, has been shown here to distinguish every graph for n≥7. Proving that a universal finite list of such signatures separates all graphs would be far stronger and is not claimed.

The immediate next mathematical experiment is to find the first n and pair of nonisomorphic graphs with matching (D,T,P₄), and classify what new deck-reconstructible induced-subgraph statistic separates that pair. That work must use independently checked census, rather than extrapolating this n=6 result.
