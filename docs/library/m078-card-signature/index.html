RESEARCH DOSSIERS

M078 — a sharp finite boundary for paired card-motif signatures

Status 2026-11-01: exact finite computational classification; independently replayed card statistics and Burnside class counts. Mathematical novelty has not been established; this is not a proof of the reconstruction conjecture. Bounded research claim #130. The separate O-M078-01 / induced-P4 proposal is not discharged by this work.

Question#

How much of a graph's vertex-deleted deck do we need to distinguish small graphs without comparing the full graph isomorphism types of the cards?

For a finite simple graph G, write D(G) = {G-v : v in V(G)} as a multiset, counting repeated cards. For a graph H, let:

  • d(H): sorted multiset of vertex degrees;
  • t(H): number of (not necessarily induced) triangles;
  • q(H): number of (not necessarily induced) four-cycles, each counted once.

Consider two deck-computable signatures, with each card's statistics paired before sorting:

S3(G) = multiset_{v in V(G)} (d(G-v), t(G-v))
S4(G) = multiset_{v in V(G)} (d(G-v), t(G-v), q(G-v))

These are much smaller summaries than the full isomorphism types of the cards. Do not replace the paired multiset with separate unpaired histograms: that is a different invariant and is not tested here.

Three exact finite assertions#

  1. Completeness of S3 through seven vertices. Among all finite simple graphs on each fixed order 3 <= n <= 7, S3 separates every graph isomorphism class. It is not assumed complete at larger orders.
  2. Sharp obstruction at eight vertices. Two explicitly listed, triangle-free graphs of order eight share S3, but one has two four-cycles and the other has one. They are not isomorphic. This is a counterexample to S3 as a complete signature, not to the graph reconstruction conjecture.
  3. Completeness of S4 through eight vertices. At each fixed order 3 <= n <= 8, S4 separates every graph isomorphism class.

All three assertions follow from the finite enumeration certificates below. They are not universal structural theorems and have not received independent specialist mathematical review.

"Excess" is the number of nonisomorphic classes minus the number of signature values. It is not necessarily the number of colliding pairs.

Constructive completeness: the project's graph-census generator builds one representative for each isomorphism class at orders 3 through 7. Its separate Burnside count and a second canonical-labelling check already certify the smaller census. For order 8, delete any vertex from an arbitrary graph; its seven-vertex remainder is isomorphic to one of those 1,044 representatives. That graph is therefore isomorphic to one of the 1,044 × 128 = 133,632 extensions formed by connecting a new vertex to every subset of the old seven. Some extensions are isomorphic; that is harmless because only distinct signatures are counted.

Independently, Burnside's lemma counts unlabeled n-vertex graphs as

(1/n!) * sum over permutations p in S_n of 2^(orbits of p on unordered vertex pairs).

It gives 12,346 classes at n=8. A signature is invariant under relabeling, so its number of distinct values can never exceed the number of classes. The exact search finds 12,346 distinct S4 values in the complete extension cover. Equality gives injectivity up to isomorphism at n=8. The same counting argument applies to S3 through n=7. The executable checker also stores SHA-256 fingerprints of sorted signature values; hashes detect drift but are not the mathematical justification.

Eight-vertex witness: S3 fails, S4 detects the difference#

Vertices are integers 0,...,7. In the fixed edge-list format [u,v] with u<v, graph A has edges:

(0,4) (0,7) (1,4) (1,5) (1,7)
(2,5) (2,6) (3,5) (3,6) (4,6)

Graph B has edges:

(0,4) (0,7) (1,4) (1,5)
(2,5) (2,6) (2,7) (3,5) (3,6) (4,6)

Both have degree sequence (2,2,2,2,3,3,3,3), ten edges and zero triangles. Their full paired S3 multisets agree exactly, including repeated cards (see the JSON certificate). But A has two four-cycles and B has one, which proves they are nonisomorphic without relying on a canonical-label algorithm. This discrepancy is itself reconstructible from the full deck: each four-cycle survives deletion of exactly n-4 vertices, so q(G) = sum_v q(G-v)/(n-4) for n>4. The pair is therefore not a same-deck counterexample. S4 distinguishes them.

Independent checks#

python3 scripts/deck_signature_frontier.py --write
python3 scripts/deck_signature_frontier.py --check         # exhaustive two-path card recheck
python3 scripts/deck_signature_frontier.py --check --quick # still exhaustive signature and Burnside counts
python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -p test_deck_signature_frontier.py -v

The search path uses bitset adjacency, deletion identities for degree and triangle counts, and common-neighbor counts for C4 in each card. The independent card checker physically constructs each deleted edge set, recomputes degree sequences from incident edges, enumerates triples for triangles and three possible cyclic orders per quadruple for C4. Its full mode checks every generated input independently. The isomorphism-class count is a distinct Burnside orbit calculation and does not depend on interpreting the signature. The tests inject a mutated edge, invalid edge lists, and vertex relabelings as negative/positive controls.

The frozen data live in data/card_motif_signatures.json. An accepted proof of a stronger reconstruction theorem would still need an independent scope and novelty review.

Prior art and novelty boundary#

  • Brendan McKay, Reconstruction of small graphs and digraphs (Australasian Journal of Combinatorics 83, 2022), computationally verifies full graph reconstruction through 13 vertices. This result is about a weaker compression of the full deck and does not extend his reconstruction bound.
  • Kelly's classical reconstruction observations apply to subgraph counts; degree sequences are also reconstructible from cards. See Wendy Myrvold, The degree sequence is reconstructible from n−1 cards (Discrete Mathematics 102, 1992).
  • Separate work on induced P4 counts addresses a different invariant and at most six vertices in its initial PR.

I did not find an exact published formulation of this paired S3/S4 signature threshold in a targeted search; absence from a search is not evidence of priority. Treat it as a reproducible mathematical candidate requiring external literature and specialist review.

Next research step: test S4 at nine vertices with a complete isomorph-free generator or find a small independent collision. Do not announce a universal bound or change data/solved.json, the evidence state of M078, or the O-M078-01 obligation on these data.
