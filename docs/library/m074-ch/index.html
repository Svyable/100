M074 — Caccetta–Häggkvist: a certified finite frontier

Intake date: 2026-10-31. Portfolio evidence state: needs_review. No new unrestricted proof is claimed. The finite digraph census below reproduces known mathematics; the 18-vertex SAT artifact is an encoding only.

Later finite closure: direct elementary proof (2026-10-31)#

A self-contained saturated-pivot contradiction now excludes all oriented directed-triangle-free 18-vertex digraphs with minimum outdegree six, without requiring an UNSAT certificate. Its independent six-vertex enumeration has a seven-vertex nonvacuity control. This finite conclusion was reported earlier by an independent DRAT-based project, and no novelty, full conjecture resolution, or fixed-r=6 all-orders proof is claimed. The SAT encodings below are retained as frozen independent replay/audit targets; they are no longer prerequisites for this finite case once the written argument is accepted.

Further finite exclusion: (21,7) by seven-vertex rigidity#

The elementary (21,7) dossier uses a seven-vertex degree lemma and a global 145-versus-147 arc-count contradiction to exclude triangle-free, 7-outregular digraphs of order 21, without assuming an SAT/UNSAT verdict. Earlier independent DRAT certificates for that same finite case are reported externally; no novelty or unrestricted Caccetta–Häggkvist claim is made. The general-conjecture evidence state and frozen (18,6) CNF records do not change.

Regular (24,8) case ruled out; irregular branch remains#

A root-neighborhood cross-cut proof excludes the 8-in/8-out-regular 24-vertex, directed-triangle-free orientation. Any hypothetical minimum-outdegree-eight counterexample at this order must, after deletion of surplus outgoing arcs, have a vertex of indegree exactly nine, which must itself have an outneighbor of indegree at most seven. The eight-neighbor maximum internal arc count is sharply 21. This is not a proof of the full finite (24,8) minimum-outdegree statement. The same finite statement was previously reported computationally by an independent team. No novelty, general CH resolution, or registry-status promotion is asserted.

Exact statement#

A finite loopless digraph on n vertices with minimum outdegree r>=1 must contain a directed cycle of length at most ceil(n/r). A two-way pair is a directed 2-cycle and automatically satisfies this bound, so a counterexample may be assumed to be an oriented graph (no digons).

References: EGRES open-problem statement; AI Mathematics Workshop summary. Historical fixed-r results include r=2,3,4,5; a general published directed-triangle threshold is approximately 0.3465n. For the density question, n=18,r=6 is a natural next finite challenge not directly covered by that numerical threshold (6/18 = 1/3).

Status qualification: recent externally posted manuscripts claim results for the fixed-r=6 problem. Their hypotheses, exact certificates, and independent acceptance require specialist review; do not treat this instance as independently confirmed open or claim novelty if their proof holds. See the external manuscript and proposed certificates as a claim to audit, not an accepted theorem.

First substantial fixed-size target: (n=18, r=6)#

For n=18,r=6, the desired bound is ceil(18/6)=3. A hypothetical counterexample is an oriented digraph with minimum outdegree 6 and no directed triangle. An exact Boolean variable x_uv denotes the arc u->v. The generated CNF contains:

Clause familyCountMeaning
One negative 2-clause per unordered pair153No digons
Two negative 3-clauses per triple1,632No directed triangles
Each 12-subset of 17 outgoing candidates, per vertex111,384Minimum outdegree at least 6
Total113,169306 arc variables

The minimum-outdegree clauses are logically equivalent to the outdegree bound: if a vertex has at most 5 outgoing arcs, there is a set of 12 other vertices with no outgoing arc, making one of the clauses false. Conversely, at least 6 outgoing arcs intersect every 12-subset.

Generated CNF size: 5,125,375 bytes. A signed SAT assignment would be a finite counterexample and must pass a separate graph verifier. An UNSAT solver result is not a rigorous proof until independently verified via a proof trace (e.g. LRAT with a trusted checker), including every symmetry-breaking assumption. No such SAT or UNSAT certificate has yet been obtained in this repository.

The generator tests prove semantic equivalence for every arc assignment for small exact cases (n=3,r=1) and (n=4,r=2), checking the CNF against the independent graph-level definition. This tests the encoding logic, not 18-vertex satisfiability.

Proof-derived reduction: exact in/out regularity and root symmetry#

A self-contained six-vertex counting lemma and 18-vertex regularity theorem show that any hypothetical counterexample at (18,6), once reduced to exact outdegree six, must also have indegree six at every vertex. After relabeling a root, its six outgoing, six incoming and five nonadjacent neighbors can be pinned to fixed blocks without losing a counterexample.

The separate scripts/ch_regularity.py emits a reproducible stronger CNF with 3,834 variables and 15,139 clauses, hash 16448ff34866b74e7b98ea38e599841363503de88590eca564ae29e2b9891d35. This is an equisatisfiable structural reduction, not a SAT/UNSAT determination or a claim of originality. It leaves the frozen direct and compact CNF artifacts untouched. The next validation gate remains a solver trace independently checked against the exact generated CNF, with prior-art audit first.
