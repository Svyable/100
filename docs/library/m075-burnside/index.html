RESEARCH DOSSIERS

M075: Burnside-certified full-support orbit counts through n=5

Exact finite result reproduced; no new Frankl theorem. This is an independent way to compute the previously published five-element orbit count, complementing the canonical-minimum construction in M075_CANONICAL.md.

Statement#

Let (\mathcal U_n) be the finite set of all nonempty union-closed families (F\subseteq 2^{[n]}) whose union is exactly ([n]). The symmetric group (S_n) acts on these families by relabeling ground elements. Compute the number of orbits (|\mathcal U_5/S_5|), without canonicalizing each of the millions of labeled families individually.

The exact known answer is 28,960, listed in OEIS A326907. We obtain this by an independently implemented group fixed-point calculation.

Lemma A: a fixed family is a union of subset-orbits#

For (\pi\in S_n), let (\langle\pi\rangle) act on (2^{[n]}). A family satisfies (\pi(F)=F) if and only if its membership is constant on every cycle (orbit) of the induced action on subsets. Proof: the forward implication follows by repeatedly applying (\pi) and (\pi^{-1}); the converse follows by applying (\pi) to the union of whole cycles. Thus we can decide one orbit of subsets at a time, not one subset at a time.

Each subset orbit has a single cardinality because permutations preserve the size of a set. Begin with the one-point subset orbit (\{[n]\}), mandatory for full-support union-closed families. Process all other subset orbits in decreasing cardinality, with a deterministic numeric tie-break.

When an orbit (B) is added, require that every union (A\cup S) with (S\in B) and (A) in the augmented family belongs to that augmented family. Exclusion does not alter the current family.

Soundness: after each accepted inclusion the family remains union-closed; invariance and full support are automatic because complete orbits are added and ([n]) remains present.

Completeness: take any (\pi)-invariant full-support union-closed family (F) and follow the decisions for its subset orbits. If an orbit (B\subset F) is to be included, all required unions lie in (F). A required union outside (B) has strictly greater size than its contributing (S\in B), unless it equals (S); hence its orbit was processed earlier and included. The same applies to unions of two members of (B). No required inclusion is pruned, so every fixed family is reached. A unique orbit decision vector generates each family exactly once. QED.

Lemma B: only cycle types need to be counted#

Conjugate permutations (\pi'=\sigma\pi\sigma^{-1}) fix equally many families: relabeling (F\mapsto\sigma(F)) is a bijection from (\operatorname{Fix}(\pi)) onto (\operatorname{Fix}(\pi')), preserving union closure and full support. Elements of (S_n) are conjugate precisely when they have the same cycle-type partition of (n). Hence only seven representative cycle types exist in (S_5).

Burnside's lemma now gives

[ |\mathcal U_n/S_n| =\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\pi\in S_n}|\operatorname{Fix}(\pi)|. ]

The identity's fixed count is the independent labeled full-support enumeration in frankl_canonical.py. For each other cycle type we enumerate the complete-orbit choices using Lemma A.

Reproduce#

python3 scripts/frankl_burnside.py --check

python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -p 'test_frankl_burnside.py' -v
  • Implementation reconstructs the subset orbits and all seven (S_5) conjugacy classes.
  • Frozen integer certificate pins every class weight and fixed-family count; CI rejects drift.
  • Adversarial tests compare fixed-point enumeration with brute-force (n=3), compare (n=4) Burnside output with direct canonicalization, and reject non-permutations and missing within-orbit unions.

This is a more efficient counting method than testing a full canonical minimum for every labeled family. It does not establish a particular measured 10x speedup, and the known n=5 result is already far inside the published cases of Frankl's conjecture. The unresolved next step remains a genuine structural lemma beyond known special cases, with independent mathematical review and exact quantifier scope before any claim is promoted.
