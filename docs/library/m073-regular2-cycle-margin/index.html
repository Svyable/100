M073: a quantitative Sidorenko margin on an entire graphon subfamily

Claim #123, November 1, 2026.

This note proves a bounded, author-derived mathematical statement, not merely checks finitely many hosts. Its underlying even-subgraph identity is the classical high-temperature expansion of the Ising model, not our invention; see Friedli–Velenik, The Ising Model, Section 3.5, equation (3.45). The specific numeric margin and special graph coefficient counts below were independently derived in this project. Their global novelty and the argument's specialist acceptance have not been established.

The graph (H) is exactly the 35-vertex, 66-edge incidence graph of the 22 triples on 13 points from the September 2026 OpenAI Sidorenko preprint. Its exact triple table is frozen, source-identified and SHA-256 checked at data/reference/sidorenko_faces.json. The manuscript claims an existential finite host violating Sidorenko; the independent finite-host witness gate in this repository remains open.

Independent complementary extension: M073_MONOTONE_TWO_BLOCK.md proves a quantitative real-parameter inequality for all ordered asymmetric two-block graphons with arbitrary class masses. Neither theorem covers the entire nonmonotone two-block region; the classical method and independent novelty boundary remain explicit.

Exact statement#

Proposition (balanced, equal-diagonal, two-step graphons). Let (a,b\in[0,1]) and let (W) be the symmetric graphon on two measurable classes of measure (1/2) each, having block matrix

[ W=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\b&a\end{pmatrix}. ]

For (p=(a+b)/2>0) and (r=(a-b)/(a+b)), the fixed graph (H) satisfies

[ \boxed{ t(H,W)\ \ge\ p^{66}\big(1+33r^4+88r^6\big) \ \ge\ p^{66}. } ]

The first inequality is strict when (a\ne b). Equality in either inequality holds exactly when (a=b), including the separate (p=0) case (a=b=0), when both sides vanish. No restriction on (a,b) to rational values or a finite test grid is needed.

The same method, for every finite bipartite graph (F) with (m) edges and (c_4,c_6) simple cycles of lengths four and six, proves

[ t(F,W) \ge p^m(1+c_4r^4+c_6r^6). ]

This general inequality is a straightforward application of the known even-subgraph identity; do not treat it as an independently novel theorem. Our application computes the previously unrecorded-in-this-project coefficients ((c_4,c_6)=(33,88)) for the source-pinned (H).

Proof#

Write independent block labels as Rademacher variables (\sigma_v\in\{-1,+1\}). Then (W(\sigma_u,\sigma_v)=p(1+r\sigma_u\sigma_v)), and by expanding one factor per edge,

[ \frac{t(F,W)}{p^m} =\mathbb{E}\prod_{uv\in E(F)}(1+r\sigma_u\sigma_v) =\sum_{S\subseteq E(F)} r^{|S|} \prod_{v\in V(F)}\mathbb E[\sigma_v^{\deg_S(v)}] =\sum_{\substack{S\subseteq E(F)\ \deg_S(v)\equiv0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 2)\,\forall v}} r^{|S|}. \tag{1} ]

The expectation vanishes if any selected degree is odd; otherwise it is one. For bipartite (F), with color class (L), an even-degree edge subset satisfies (|S|=\sum_{v\in L}\deg_S(v)\equiv0\pmod2). Thus all surviving powers of (r) are even and nonnegative even when (r<0). The empty subset supplies 1. In a simple bipartite graph, a nonempty Eulerian subgraph with four edges is exactly a four-cycle; with six edges it is exactly a six-cycle (there are no two-cycles or odd cycles). Keeping just those terms gives the general inequality.

We now derive the exact source-specific coefficients without invoking a numerical graphon search. Define the point skeleton (K) on 13 points: two points are adjacent if they occur together in a listed triple. An explicit finite audit of the pinned 22 triples yields:

  1. (K) has 33 edges, and each skeleton edge lies in exactly two listed triples.
  2. (K) has exactly 22 triangles; they are precisely the 22 listed triples (there are no additional triangles). This checks all (\binom{13}{3}=286) point triples.

Every four-cycle of the incidence graph is specified by a skeleton edge and the two distinct incident face vertices. Therefore (c_4=33).

Every six-cycle is specified by three point vertices (u,v,w) forming a skeleton triangle, together with three distinct face vertices, one containing each of (uv,vw,wu). There are two possible faces for each of the triangle's three edges. One choice is the common triangle face, the other its distinct neighboring face. Of the (2^3=8) assignments, exactly four select the common face zero or one times; these and only these give three distinct face vertices. (The neighboring faces are mutually distinct, because no other listed triple contains all three points.) Hence (c_6=22\times4=88).

The empty, four-edge, and six-edge Eulerian subsets contribute (1+33r^4+88r^6) in (1). Every omitted term is nonnegative. As (c_4>0), the inequality is strict for (r\ne0), equivalently (a\ne b). For (p=0), both block probabilities are zero and (t(H,W)=0). This completes the proof.

Adversarial controls and exact replay#

The two coefficient counts are independently checked with:

  • a point-triple combinatorial census and the 22-triangle skeleton structural audit; and
  • a different DFS on the entire 35-vertex incidence graph that counts unoriented simple cycles once via canonical traversal.

The exact (25)-point rational subgrid (a,b\in \{0,1/4,1/2,3/4,1\}) is separately checked via integer homomorphism densities from the existing M073 face-elimination engine; four cases use its independent 66-edge factor-elimination engine too. These samples are regressions not the proof of the continuum statement.

Additional tests directly enumerate all even edge subsets of small graphs and compare the spin sum to exact pairwise graphon factors. A deliberately nonbipartite triangle with cross-only weights has (t(K_3,W)=0<p^3): dropping the bipartiteness step would make a false argument. Boolean weights, invalid probabilities, and a mutated source table fail closed. The incidence graph is independently verified connected, and its cycle-space rank (66-35+1=32) gives an endpoint checksum: at (|r|=1), the full even-subgraph polynomial evaluates to (2^{32}). This checksum does not claim that all (2^{32}) Eulerian subsets were individually enumerated.

Evidence boundary and next falsifier#

This excludes the entire equal-mass, equal-diagonal two-step graphon subfamily as a source of the claimed M073 counterexample. It does not exclude unequal diagonal entries, unequal class masses, three-plus blocks, arbitrary graphons or finite simple hosts in general. It does not prove that Sidorenko's conjecture holds, nor refute the September 2026 alleged counterexample.

Next: Attack the missing symmetry assumptions by testing (a\ne c) and unequal masses, or derive a higher-block exact rational negative witness and then a finite host. Preserve the fixed incidence pattern, exact normalization and a second independent density check. Prior-art audit of the specific quantitative bound and specialist review of this proof remain required before claiming a novel published mathematical result or changing the Solved/evidence ledgers.
