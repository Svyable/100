RESEARCH DOSSIERS

M079 — quantitative 3/8 balance candidate for chain-plus-five antichain posets

Work: O-M079-04; claim #201; canonical work-queue scope SHA-256 5629ffe92a7529266dc2a522e885ab96934a4d780f7cf38dbdf325fb9bbab844 (exploratory pre-ledger digest a9f7bf91b0171598bd03cdac0fc5e690e7b29a62dccc0796d75f06085af76b87).

Evidence classification: author-derived restricted all-orders proof candidate, using an elementary parameterized lemma plus a finite exhaustive certificate. There are two exact counting algorithms and a third, relation-matrix-based checker for the sharp finite witness. No independent human mathematical review or historical-novelty acceptance. The general one-third–two-thirds conjecture remains open; do not update the Solved ledger.

Precisely quantified statement#

Let P be a finite poset consisting of a designated chain c1 < ... < cm and five additional elements x0,...,x4. Assume:

  1. The five outsiders are pairwise incomparable.
  2. The width of P is exactly five.
  3. Every outsider is incomparable with at least one chain element.

For uniformly random linear extension L, set

delta(P) = max_(u != v) min(Pr_L(u before v), Pr_L(v before u)).

Restricted theorem candidate: delta(P) >= 3/8. The infimum for this infinite class lies in [3/8, 2/5]: a 13-element example below has exactly delta(P)=2/5. The lower bound is not asserted sharp. This is not a theorem about arbitrary width-five posets, arbitrary chain-deletion-five posets, or the unrestricted conjecture.

Parameterized gap-atom lemma (all chain lengths)#

More generally, suppose P is a chain plus k>=2 pairwise incomparable outsiders. Number the m+1 gaps in the chain 0,...,m. For outsider x, its allowed insertion gaps form a nonempty integer interval I_x, of length L. Its chain predecessors are a prefix and its chain successors a suffix, so these are all the possible gaps.

Lemma. For each allowed gap g,

Pr(x is inserted in g) <= k/(L+k-1).

Proof. Fix a linear extension Q of P-x. For each g in I_x, let t_g(Q) be the number of other outsiders of Q in gap g. Because x is incomparable with all of them, there are exactly 1+t_g ways to insert x at g, and L+sum_(h in I_x) t_h legal insertions overall. Thus the conditional mass is

(1+t_g)/(L+sum t_h) <= (1+t_g)/(L+t_g) <= k/(L+k-1),

since 0<=t_g<=k-1 and (1+t)/(L+t) is nondecreasing for L>=1. The uniform distribution on extensions of P induces a size-biased mixture of these conditional distributions over Q, but a pointwise upper bound survives the mixture. This avoids incorrectly treating Q as uniformly distributed after deleting x. QED.

Threshold consequence. For 0<alpha<1/2, if

L >= ceil(k/(1-2*alpha) - k + 1),

then x has an alpha-balanced pair with a chain vertex. Indeed, every gap atom has mass at most 1-2*alpha; the first cumulative mass at least alpha is at most 1-alpha. It is reached at a real chain vertex: the final atom is at most 1-2*alpha, so the cumulative mass before the last gap is at least 2*alpha >= alpha. (At equality the endpoint is allowed.) The pair probability is precisely this cumulative gap mass.

For alpha=1/3 the cutoff is L>=2k+1. In particular, this gives a general finite-kernel reduction for the width-k antichain-outsider class: a hypothetical counterexample must have all outsider intervals of length at most 2k.

Helly/width reduction and the quantitative finite core#

If two outsiders have disjoint gap intervals, the chain forces a comparison between them, contrary to their incomparability. Thus their intervals intersect pairwise; integer intervals satisfy Helly's property and all five share a gap. If their common intersection contained two consecutive gaps, the chain vertex between those gaps would be incomparable with all five outsiders, producing an antichain of size six. Width exactly five forces one common gap. Translate that gap to zero:

max_i a_i = min_i b_i = 0,\quad I_i=[a_i,b_i],\quad b_i-a_i+1>=2.

Conversely, these constraints construct exactly the specified class, up to chain vertices before/after the union of intervals. Such outer padding is comparable with every outsider, has a fixed place in every extension, and does not change any pair probability.

The general 1/3 kernel therefore needs at most 4k-2 chain vertices and 5k-2 total vertices: every interval has length at most 2k, so its left endpoint is at least -(2k-1) and its right endpoint at most 2k-1. This is a finite decision reduction for each fixed k, not a proof of the conjecture for every k.

For the stronger alpha=3/8 at k=5, the gap-atom bound gives

Pr(x in g) <= 5/(L+4) <= 1/4 = 1-2*(3/8) whenever L>=16.

It remains to check all five-interval multisets with lengths 2..15, each containing zero, and common intersection exactly {0}. There are N=2+3+...+15=119 possible intervals. Sorting the five interval labels removes only their permutation symmetry. The number of eligible multisets is independently given by inclusion-exclusion:

binom(123,5) - 2*binom(109,5) + binom(95,5) = 40,355,371.

Here one interval must have left endpoint zero and another right endpoint zero; these two sets of intervals are disjoint because length is at least two. The C++ enumerator checks every sorted multiset, not a sample.

Two exact integer counting recurrences#

For each multiset, the checker computes the total number E of linear extensions and every outsider–outsider and outsider–chain before-count.

  • Sequential order-ideal recurrence: state (q,mask), where q is the chain prefix already placed and mask records placed outsiders. A chain step is legal only when it does not pass an unplaced outsider's latest allowed gap; an outsider step is legal at its current allowed gap. Forward/backward path counts weight each outsider insertion. Its insertion at gap q precedes all chain vertices c_t with t>q, and precedes each as-yet-unplaced outsider.
  • Factorial gap-block recurrence: at each chain gap, choose a subset T of eligible unplaced outsiders, require every outsider whose last gap is now to be included, and weight that block by |T|!. Its subset transitions and forward/backward counts are structurally different from sequential one-element moves. For two outsiders in the same block, exactly half the |T|! permutations put one before the other. The two paths must agree on E, all ten outsider-pair counts, and every outsider's per-gap marginal vector, for every multiset.

The finite certificate compares integer numerators, not floating point: 8*max_pair min(count,E-count) >= 3*E. Both paths are run over the entire residual. Their agreement is an independent algorithmic check, not an independent person, formal kernel proof, or specialist acceptance. The largest residual chain has 28 vertices, so even the crude extension bound 33!/28! = 28,480,320 is below signed 64-bit range.

A concrete falsifier of transferring the four-outsider constant#

Take chain gaps -4,-3,-2,-1,0,1,2,3,4 (eight chain vertices) and five pairwise incomparable outsiders with intervals

[-4,0], [-1,0], [-1,1], [0,1], [0,4].

All intervals have at least two gaps, and their common intersection is exactly zero, so width is exactly five. The poset has 1,405 linear extensions and best smaller-side numerator 562, hence

delta(P)=562/1405=2/5 < 25/61.

The 25/61 sharp candidate for four outsiders (active PR #184) therefore does not transfer verbatim to five outsiders. This is a falsifier of that stronger extrapolation, not a counterexample to the 1/3–2/3 conjecture.

A compact certificate, with outsider indices in the interval order above:

  • Outsider–outsider before-counts in lexicographic pair order: 977, 1075, 1200, 1272, 880, 1083, 1200, 880, 1075, 977.
  • Per-gap extension weights (gaps -4,...,4): x0: 183,183,183,294,562,0,0,0,0; x1: 0,0,0,492,913,0,0,0,0; x2: 0,0,0,355,695,355,0,0,0; x3: 0,0,0,0,913,492,0,0,0; x4: 0,0,0,0,562,294,183,183,183.

The weights in every row sum to 1405. Prefix sums give all outsider–chain probabilities; no pair exceeds the smaller-side numerator 562. In particular x4<c5 occurs in exactly 562 extensions.

The test suite rebuilds the actual 13-element relation matrix, counts linear extensions by a third bitmask order-ideal recurrence over all vertices, and recomputes each pair count after adding that relation. It does not use either gap-state recurrence.

Exploratory search provenance (not proof evidence)#

Before the exhaustive certificate, a seeded Python gap-state search sampled 20,000 five-interval tuples with lengths 2–9 and endpoints containing zero (seed 97129); 10,107 had singleton common intersection and the best sampled balance was 2197/5110, not a counterexample to 25/61. Starting instead from the four-outsider sharp tuple [-1,0],[0,1],[-1,1],[-4,0], a deterministic scan of every fifth interval of lengths 2–30 containing zero found 2/5 at [0,4]. A seven-layer bounded neighbor/beam search around that witness inspected 3,025 distinct normalized tuples and did not improve it. Those searches are heuristic and incomplete; no inference about the infinite class or finite minimum is drawn from them. The exact witness is reproducible via m079_chain5_kernel --instance "-4:0,-1:0,-1:1,0:1,0:4" after compilation; the full census is the actual proof obligation.

Reproduction and adversarial controls#

From the repository root (Python 3.12+, C++17 compiler):

python3 scripts/m079_chain5_kernel.py --check --json
python3 scripts/m079_chain5_kernel.py --smoke --json
python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -p 'test_m079_chain5_kernel.py' -v
python3 scripts/regen.py --check --fast
python3 -m unittest discover -s tests

The full check enumerates 40,355,371 normalized interval multisets, checks every one with both recurrences, and compares the count to a separate inclusion-exclusion formula. The finite minimum 2/5 is attained by exactly one normalized interval multiset. The code prints a deterministic 64-bit FNV transcript digest b1671adf73c7eb56 for drift detection (not a cryptographic authenticity claim). A 21,250-case smoke check is for quick development, not a substitute for the full certificate.

Adversarial tests include a deliberate same-gap factorial-order mutation (which the two recurrences must disagree on), an invalid width-six shared multi-gap example, a singleton-gap outsider, disjoint intervals, reversed endpoints, all 120 outsider permutations of the witness, order duality, translation, the exact 15/16-gap analytic boundary, and the false naive product-of-interval-lengths count. A separate explicit-poset checker verifies the witness and 20 seeded small instances.

Literature and novelty audit (checked 2026-11-01)#

  • Brightwell and Wright, The 1/3–2/3 Conjecture for 5-Thin Posets, SIAM J. Discrete Math. 5 (1992): bounded incomparability degree, whereas this chain-plus-five class contains outsiders incomparable with arbitrarily many chain vertices.
  • Zaguia, The 1/3–2/3 Conjecture for N-Free Ordered Sets (2012): different forbidden-subposet condition; no N-free assumption here.
  • Olson and Sagan, On the 1/3–2/3 Conjecture (2018): established families and dimension-two subcases, not an identified exact chain-plus-five quantitative theorem.
  • Chen, A Family of Partially Ordered Sets with Small Balance Constant (2018): general posets can have balance approaching 0.3488999, so 3/8 cannot hold universally.
  • Aires and Kahn, Balancing Extensions in Posets of Large Width (2025), and Aires, Proof of the Kahn Saks Conjecture (September 2026): asymptotic large-width results; neither abstract establishes this fixed-width-five quantitative statement.
  • Aires, Chan, Pak and Panova, Breaking the Infinite Barrier in the 1/3–2/3 Conjecture (September 2026): a universal epsilon improvement over the 1995 bound, still below the conjectured 1/3 and not an identified fixed-width-five 3/8 theorem.
  • Gupta, Balance Constants, Majority Cycles, and the Gold Partition Conjecture through Fourteen Elements (July 2026): finite order-14 work, not the present unbounded-chain statement.
  • The separately claimed chain-plus-four candidate has 25/61 and a different residual. Its scope, files, tests and landing workflow are not modified here.

A targeted source search did not identify the exact 3/8 bound for this class, but absence from search is not evidence of historical novelty. An expert should check older near-chain, dimension-two, partition-poset, interval-order and width-five literature in full, not merely abstracts.

Failed avenues, assumptions and next falsifiers#

The original 1/3 gap cutoff (L>=11) gives a smaller 1,426,356-case finite kernel, but only the weaker conclusion; the 3/8 cutoff (L>=16) requires the 40,355,371-case residual. The naive prod |I_i| count is false because outsiders sharing a gap can be ordered in factorially many ways (witness: 300 != 1405). The hoped-for transfer of the chain-plus-four 25/61 bound is falsified exactly by the 13-element example. This method does not prove 2/5 for the unbounded class: its gap-atom threshold for 2/5 would require L>=21, leaving a much larger residual.

Material hypotheses: finite poset; specified chain; exactly five outsiders; outsiders form an antichain; width exactly five; every outsider has a chain incomparability; uniform distribution on linear extensions. If outsider comparabilities are added, the insertion-slot formula can fail. If width is six, the common-gap singleton reduction fails. If an outsider has only one gap, it is outside the finite enumeration. No claim about those excluded classes follows.

Next falsifier: a concrete valid five-interval multiset with 8*best<3*E; a mismatch in the independent full 40m-case recurrence; a relation-matrix mismatch; an omitted interval multiset; an overflow or off-by-one in the gap cutoff; or a primary historical source proving the same or stronger class bound. Obtain a human scope/proof review and an independent specialist novelty audit before any acceptance or solved-status change. The current result is a candidate awaiting those checks.
