M093 — exact 4-arcs in PG(3,q), q ≤ 9 (known finite reproduction)

Date: 2026-11-01. Claim: #114. Work item: O-M093-01, scope digest e0d611142957b847. Classification: published/elementary finite mathematics, reproduced by a new exact implementation. Not a new bound, a general MDS proof, an independently reviewed result, or a Solved-ledger event.

Primary literature and exact question#

A 4-arc in PG(3,q) is a set of distinct projective points represented by nonzero vectors in GF(q)^4 such that every four are linearly independent. The finite question is the largest number of points in such a set for each prime power q ≤ 9 (there is no field GF(6)).

  • Simeon Ball, On sets of vectors of a finite vector space in which every subset of basis size is a basis, J. Eur. Math. Soc. 14 (2012), 733–748. The prime-field MDS theorem is background for q=5,7; its stated range must not be applied without checking hypotheses to q=2,3 or nonprime fields.
  • Gerzson Kéri, Types of superregular matrices and the number of n-arcs and complete n-arcs in PG(r,q), J. Combin. Designs 14 (2006), 363–390. The published computational classification covers r=3 for q=5,7,8,9, among other cases.
  • Simeon Ball and Michel Lavrauw, Arcs in finite projective spaces, EMS Surveys Math. Sci. 6 (2019), 133–172 (published January 2020): geometric and MDS context and the scope of prime-field results.

These are known maximum sizes; this is a new replay with a simple exhaustive frame-normalized engine, not a specialist review of the source papers.

Why the finite search is exhaustive#

Any 5-point 4-arc can be taken to the ordered projective frame e1,e2,e3,e4,u, with u=(1,1,1,1), by a projective invertible coordinate change. Indeed, the first four form a basis and the fifth has four nonzero basis coordinates; independently rescale those basis vectors to make the fifth coordinates all one. All 5-frames are therefore equivalent for existence questions.

For any sixth point x, the four determinants using x and three basis vectors require all four x coordinates to be nonzero. Normalize x1=1. The determinants using x, u and any two basis vectors require the four coordinates of x to be pairwise distinct. Thus every possible sixth point is exactly one of the (q-2)(q-3)(q-4) vectors (1,a,b,c), with a,b,c distinct and drawn from GF(q) minus {0,1}. For q=2,3,4 there is no sixth point, while the frame itself exists.

For q≥5, the engine enumerates increasing-index subsets of these candidate points. Every newly formed 4-subset has k=1,2,3 or 4 candidate points and 4-k frame points. The recursive search explicitly checks all such determinants; its pruning removes only a subset already failing one. A complete UNSAT result for size n+1 excludes every larger arc because any larger arc would contain an (n+1)-arc. A cutoff is labelled UNKNOWN, never UNSAT.

Finite fields are represented exactly by GF(2), GF(3), GF(5), GF(7), GF(4)=GF(2)[t]/(t²+t+1), GF(8)=GF(2)[t]/(t³+t+1), GF(9)=GF(3)[t]/(t²+1). Determinants use finite-field elimination. The separate Leibniz permutation formula checks positive witnesses and sample determinants without using elimination.

All seven exclusion searches returned UNSAT from complete deterministic enumeration, not from a SAT solver or a separately verified proof trace. Each lower-bound witness was independently checked by the Leibniz determinant formula; for q≥4 the script also checks the normal rational curve with q+1 points. The test suite separately brute-forces all 5- and 6-subsets of the 15 projective points of PG(3,2), obtaining 168 five-arcs and zero six-arcs. These are known finite facts.

Reproduction, negative controls, and limitations#

python3 scripts/pg3_arcs.py
python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -p 'test_pg3_arcs.py' -v
python3 scripts/regen.py --check
python3 -m unittest discover -s tests

The eight focused tests exercise field distributivity/inverses, non-field rejection for q=6, candidate counts, independent determinant formulas, brute-force PG(3,2), all seven maxima, duplicate/coordinate tampering, and a deliberate q=9 search cutoff returning UNKNOWN.

The field arithmetic is shared between the two determinant paths, and the negative search uses a single implementation of frame normalization. Therefore these tests are not independent human mathematical review, nor do they prove correctness of an external classification database. A specialist should check the frame reduction, the small-field tables, the Kéri baseline and the exact q=8/9 exclusion against an independently implemented finite-field engine. No proof-obligation discharge is recorded.

Next falsifier: independently enumerate frame extensions in GF(8) and GF(9) using a different field representation and determinant algorithm; look for a 10-point 4-arc in PG(3,8) or an 11-point 4-arc in PG(3,9), or any missed 4-subset/candidate under the normalization. A genuine counterexample would invalidate this replay; absent one, outside scope review is still required before accepting O-M093-01.
