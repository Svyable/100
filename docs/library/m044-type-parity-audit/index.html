The assembly section expressly selects an outcome of a conditioned random matching model instead of enumerating the matching edges. The paper gives a construction by existence, not a particular edge-labeled pair of graphs. Consequently it supplies neither a concrete list of cone relators nor concrete root-to-vertex words for the proposed `a,b,c` in `F_2[G]`. The abstract definitions in Proposition "Parity criterion" are not a replayable input file. We cannot truthfully report either exact published-support group-ring multiplication or an independent witness. This is an extractability gap, not a counterexample to the manuscript's theorem or a proof that no finite witness exists.

What was checked exactly#

`python3 scripts/m044_type_audit.py --check` implements finite field arithmetic in `F_2[x]/(x^7+x+1)` (checking all 127 nonzero elements have multiplicative inverses), enumerates all `q^2+q+1 = 16,513` normalized projective points and lines for `q=128`, and enumerates the `16,513 × 129 = 2,130,177` incidences. Each line has 129 distinct points and each point lies on 129 lines. For two distinct lines the unique projective intersection follows from rank two of independent linear forms; the checker also cross-product-checks deterministic pairs. These are finite computations and elementary projective geometry, not a review of the random matching estimates.

The seven complements of the Fano-plane lines have size four, meet pairwise in two elements, and each of the seven extra letters belongs to four complements. The ordinary part of two prescribed label sets intersects in 129 points for the same line, and one for different lines. The extra part contributes an even intersection except for the distinguished root with itself, where it contributes seven. Thus the exact parity hypotheses of Proposition "Parity criterion" hold at the type level.

As a small, explicit type-only allocation, take `m=5`, put 161 vertices of each of the seven extra types in distinct line classes in `A`, 129 of each in distinct line classes in `B`, and place the unique root in an unused `A` line class. There are enough line classes (`7×161+1=1,128 < 16,513` and `7×129=903 < 16,513`). The seven extra letters each occur `4×161+1=645` times in `A` and `4×129=516` times in `B`, exactly matching each ordinary letter's `129m=645` and `129(m−1)=516` occurrences. The type allocation has 82,565 `A` vertices and 66,052 `B` vertices. No edges were matched: `m=5` is not asserted to meet the manuscript's asymptotic girth, spherical-arrangement, root-protection, or torsion-freeness conditions.

The tests reject a modified Fano complement, a duplicated complement, an invalid residue class for `m`, and a reducible polynomial `x^7+1`. The projective census is a different arithmetic path from the Fano combinatorics; neither is a second group-ring multiplication.

Precise unverified dependencies#

  1. An explicit pair of labeled matching graphs with exact roots and a verified no-arrangements / root-protection certificate is absent.
  2. Without that graph, the finite group presentation and the exact supports of `a,b,c` cannot be extracted for independent word reduction.
  3. The manuscript's probabilistic and topological arguments, including torsion-freeness and nontriviality of the proposed `c`, were not independently checked here.
  4. Therefore `ab=1`, `ac=0`, `c≠0`, `ba≠1`, and the M049 nonsoficity consequence remain unverified claims in this audit. Do not change `data/solved.json`, claim-review status, or obligation status.

Next decisive falsifier / replay contract#

Obtain a pinned concrete pair `Γ_A, Γ_B` with every vertex's signed outgoing labels, matched endpoint, two roots, and the finite presentation derived from the cones. Preserve input hashes. Independently check immersion, all type parities, girth, no forbidden arrangements and root protection; extract exact path words for the group-ring supports. Then use two genuinely distinct group-ring multiplication/reduction implementations to test `ab=1` and `ac=0`, and independently certify `c≠0`. A trace with `ab≠1`, `ba=1`, a support mismatch, or a failed root-protection check would be decisive against that particular candidate. A failed search or a type-only success is not a refutation or verification of the claimed existence theorem.
