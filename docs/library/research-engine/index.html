METHOD & GOVERNANCE

Proof-driven research engine

This is an operational research ledger, not an automated theorem-proving claim. It separates mathematical ambition, proof readiness, reproducible evidence, and confirmed full resolutions. Last program update: 2026-10-31.

The two stack ranks#

The existing editorial ranking (20–100 points) remains:

  • Mathematical impact: 30%
  • Attackability: 30%
  • Independent verifiability: 25%
  • Cross-problem method spillover: 15%

A separate execution index adds up to ten documented readiness points:

Evidence of readinessBonusMeaning
Exact research campaign brief+2The first proof obligation and falsifier are written
Allowlisted independently runnable verifier+3A checking program exists and is tested
Reproduced known mathematical benchmark+2Established result independently reproduced; not novel
Independently reviewed restricted new theorem+3New, carefully scoped partial theorem with two independent reviews

The execution index is not a probability, and high readiness is not evidence for a general conjecture. Each factor is deliberately bounded to prevent repeated runs of the same toy case from inflating the rank. The original editorial rank stays available. It is no longer the default work order.

Dispatch score#

Readiness rewards having a verifier, including a verifier whose only remaining experiment sits inside a theorem the campaign blocker already cites. The dispatch score is the work order:

editorial score + readiness + action bonus.

Bonuses live in data/methods.json. They promote a claimed finite witness, a missing engine and a published certificate, and they penalise a dominated range extension. The bonus is a scheduling weight, not evidence and not a probability. Every problem also belongs to one shape: a shared attack template with a certificate and a falsifier. Filling a shape does not assert that the restriction is open or true. Jobs, shapes and the per-problem order are generated in APPROACH.md. The site's default sort is this dispatch order.

  • Source registry: data/problems.json
  • Research campaigns / reproducibility milestones: data/research_program.json
  • Generated execution queue: ATTACK_QUEUE.md
  • Canonical solved archive: data/solved.json

Proof work loop (10 operations, not ten speculative agents)#

  1. Precisely formulate the conjecture with quantified domain.
  2. Identify the strongest existing theorems and unresolved residue.
  3. Pick the smallest falsifiable restricted statement.
  4. Specify exact counterexample and construction certificates.
  5. Implement a separate verifier before launching costly search.
  6. Reproduce known toy results and measure verifier coverage.
  7. Search both for proof lemmas and adversarial counterexamples.
  8. Preserve exact inputs, scripts, resource usage, and negative results.
  9. Require independent mathematical and scope review of any novel claim.
  10. Add verified methods to the library and rerank the work queue.

A failed search or an unreviewed manuscript is logged as a research lead, not a theorem. Mathematical scope review is different from code correctness.

A probe is a short module containing PROBE metadata, search(params), an independent check(params, result), and scope(params). The checker uses a different code path from the search: Miller–Rabin instead of a sieve, combinatorial incidence axioms instead of field arithmetic, Burnside or orbit-counting completeness certificates, and a field rebuilt from the certified modulus. python3 scripts/lab.py runs every probe, records a deterministic ledger in data/lab_results.json (summaries and certificate SHA-256 only), and regenerates LAB.md. --check reruns probes and fails on any drift. --emit writes full certificates and --replay re-verifies them, so an outside reviewer can check results without trusting the search.

Probes are allowlisted in scripts/probes/__init__.py; data files cannot add executables. Each probe has to be a verifier of its problem's campaign and have a reproduced_known milestone, and research.py enforces this. Every problem also carries a modality. Each computable problem (finitely refutable, finite parameter, existence family) must either have a verifier or appear in data/engine_roadmap.json, and the lab enforces this too.

Exact verification baselines#

python3 scripts/ramsey.py demo
python3 scripts/union_closed.py exhaustive --n 4
python3 scripts/digraph.py exhaustive --n 4
python3 scripts/digraph_orbits.py --n 5
python3 scripts/ch_sat.py cnf --n 18 --r 6 --output /tmp/ch18-6.cnf
python3 scripts/lab.py --level deep

The first four reproduce known statements in finite domains. The fifth command generates a SAT problem encoding only, not a result. The sixth reruns all twelve lab probes at their larger size; these are also known statements. They do not resolve Ramsey R(5,5), Frankl, or Caccetta–Häggkvist in general. Each script is independently exercised in the test suite and in a weekly GitHub Actions reproducibility workflow (when Actions scheduling is enabled by GitHub). The periodic job reruns existing tests—it does not research new literature, discover a lemma, or verify newly published claims on its own.

Promote a result to Solved#

Only a full proof or counterexample of the exact archived statement qualifies. The archive is intentionally empty initially, and may grow only through reviewed full resolutions. Every entry must include:

  1. A stable ID removed from the 100 active candidates (without recycling it).
  2. The precise original statement and proved/refuted outcome.
  3. A published full proof URL and dated acceptance record.
  4. Two distinct independent external review URLs with the result checked for the same exact theorem scope.
  5. Verification notes and a unique new active candidate replacing the solved one.
  6. If the prior target had a campaign, move it to the resolved_archived stage so its research history remains accessible without entering the active execution queue.

Schema and consistency checks reject entries lacking those references or whose old ID remains active. These checks cannot replace real specialist judgment; a maintainer must investigate the independence and correctness of the cited assessments.

A restricted new theorem goes in the milestone ledger, with review evidence, but cannot enter Solved until the entire conjecture is accepted as resolved. A reproduced previously known theorem also stays out of Solved.

Change management#

python3 scripts/build_site.py
python3 scripts/lab.py --check

python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -v

Do not hand-edit generated RANKED.md, ATTACK_QUEUE.md, LAB.md, data/lab_results.json or docs/data/portfolio.json. CI checks all of them for consistency. The site exposes evidence and ranked research priorities, never asserts a universal proof merely because a finite verifier passed.
