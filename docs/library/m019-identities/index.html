RESEARCH DOSSIERS

M019 — parametric identities from the residue mine

Dated: 2026-10-31. Scope: explicit infinite families of solutions of $4/n = 1/x + 1/y + 1/z$ on arithmetic progressions inside $n \equiv 1\pmod{24}$. Not claimed: a proof of the Erdős–Straus conjecture, novelty over classical Mordell-type coverings, or coverage of every integer $n \equiv 1\pmod{24}$.

The certified mine straus_patterns only clusters solutions for primes $n \le 8000$ with $n \equiv 1\pmod{24}$ by the residue $4x-n$. This note turns the two dominant clusters (residues 3 and 7) into polynomial identities, then adds a short list of fixed-divisor companions. The independent checker is scripts/straus_identities.py; the coverage table is the mine straus_identities.

Why this is the overlooked step#

Classical unit-fraction identities already settle every $n\not\equiv 1\pmod{24}$. Inside the hard class, a computer search keeps rediscovering the same progressions. Writing the closed form, and proving it as a polynomial identity, is the part the table does not do. Extending the verified range is refused by the dispatcher: that range already sits inside published computations.

Two primary identities#

Residue 3 on $n = 120t+97$#

For every integer $t \ge 0$ set

$$ n = 120t+97,\qquad x = 30t+25,\qquad y = (12t+10)\,n,\qquad z = (60t+50)\,n = 5y. $$

Then $4x-n = 3$ and

$$ \frac{4}{n} - \frac{1}{x} = \frac{3}{nx} = \frac{1}{y} + \frac{1}{5y} = \frac{6}{5y}. $$

The middle equality is $5y = 2nx$, which holds because $y = (2x/5)\,n$ and $x = 30t+25$ is divisible by 5. Clearing denominators gives the polynomial identity checked in code.

Residue 7 on $n = 120t+73$#

For every integer $t \ge 0$ set

$$ n = 120t+73,\qquad x = 30t+20,\qquad y = (6t+4)\,n,\qquad z = (15t+10)\,n. $$

Then $4x-n = 7$ and $1/y + 1/z = 7/(nx)$. Equivalently $y = (x/5)\,n$ and $z = (x/2)\,n$, which are integral because $x = 30t+20$.

Together these two progressions already match 65 of the 117 primes $n \le 8000$ with $n \equiv 1\pmod{24}$ in the residue mine.

Fixed-divisor companions#

Write $x = (n+r)/4$ with residue $r \in \{3,7,11\}$ and choose a positive divisor $d$ of $x^2$. Set

$$ a = \frac{x+d}{r},\qquad b = \frac{x^2/d + x}{r},\qquad y = an,\qquad z = bn. $$

The relation $(ra-x)(rb-x) = x^2$ forces $rab = x(a+b)$, which is exactly $4/n = 1/x + 1/y + 1/z$. On any arithmetic progression where $a$ and $b$ are integer polynomials in the parameter, this is again a polynomial identity.

The repository records seven such companions (moduli 168 and 264), including the composite-only progression $n = 168t+49 = 7(24t+7)$, which never contributes an odd prime but still covers an infinite hard-class progression.

Coverage certificate#

On the smoke range $N = 8000$:

  • 117 primes are $\equiv 1\pmod{24}$;
  • the nine identities cover 106 of them by closed form;
  • the 11 uncovered primes all lie in $\{1,49\}\pmod{120}$.

Run:

python3 scripts/straus_identities.py --check
python3 scripts/mine.py --mine straus_identities
python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -p 'test_straus_identities.py' -v

Evidence boundary#

  • The identities are reproduced constructive coverings, in the same spirit as classical Mordell / Sierpiński parametric solutions. Do not describe them as a new resolution of Erdős–Straus, and do not promote M019 toward Solved.
  • A finite list of polynomial identities cannot cover every $n \equiv 1\pmod{24}$; that obstruction is already the campaign blocker.
  • The next attack is therefore not a longer search. It is either a genuinely new restricted family outside these Mordell-type progressions, or a different idea for the remaining classes $n \equiv 1,49\pmod{120}$.
