# M075: an exact orbit map and closure-pruned generator

**2026-10-31 — reusable finite machinery, not a new theorem.** This addresses the
construction and quantifier gaps in Lamport obligations O-M075-01 and O-M075-02.
Frankl's full 1/2 assertion remains open. Its validity for small ground sets
is published earlier work; no novelty is claimed from our finite counts.

## Domain and encoding

Let \([n]=\{0,\ldots,n-1\}\). A *family* \(F\) is a set of distinct subsets
of \([n]\). Represent a subset \(S\) by its binary characteristic integer
\(s\), and a family by \(e(F)=\sum_{S\in F} 2^s\). In particular, the bit
positions of the family encoding index *subsets*, not individual elements.

For a permutation \(\pi\in S_n\), define
\(\pi(S)=\{\pi(i):i\in S\}\) and \(\pi(F)=\{\pi(S):S\in F\}\).
The permutation acts bijectively and satisfies
\(\pi(A\cup B)=\pi(A)\cup\pi(B)\). Therefore it preserves being
union-closed and having support \([n]\).

## Lemma 1: canonical encoding exactly identifies relabeling orbits

Define

\[
C_n(F)=\min_{\pi\in S_n} e(\pi(F)).
\]

**Claim:** For any families \(F,G\) over \([n]\),

\[
C_n(F)=C_n(G)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\exists\,\sigma\in S_n:\ G=\sigma(F).
\]

**Proof.** If \(G=\sigma(F)\), as \(\pi\) ranges over \(S_n\), so
does \(\pi\sigma\), hence the two sets of encoded images and their minima
are the same. Conversely, choose permutations \(\alpha,\beta\) attaining
the equal minima (they exist because \(S_n\) is finite).
The integer encoding is injective, so \(\alpha(F)=\beta(G)\).
Thus \(G=\beta^{-1}\alpha(F)\). QED.

No computational enumeration is needed for the abstract lemma. The
implementation [frankl_canonical.py](../scripts/frankl_canonical.py) tests
the actual bit permutation and minimum definition, including at \(n=5\),
against explicit relabelings. An independent n≤4 orbit counter is already
in [frankl_orbits.py](../scripts/mines/frankl_orbits.py).

## Lemma 2: closure-pruned descending construction is complete

Let \(F\) be nonempty, union-closed, and with support
\(\bigcup_{S\in F}S=[n]\). Since the family is finite and union-closed,
\(U=[n]\) belongs to \(F\).

Start from \(\{U\}\). Decide every other subset \(S\subsetneq U\) exactly
once in descending order of size (fixed numerical tie break). At each
step either exclude \(S\), or include it **only if**
\(S\cup A\) is already present or equals \(S\) for every existing
member \(A\).

**Soundness:** the starting singleton is union-closed. An inclusion
satisfying the stated criterion preserves closure under all pairs;
exclusion leaves the current family unchanged. Thus every emitted
family is union-closed with support \(U\).

**Completeness:** take any target \(F\) as above. Follow its
include/exclude decisions. If \(S\in F\), any union \(S\cup A\), with
previously selected \(A\in F\), also belongs to \(F\). If this union
strictly contains \(S\), it was considered earlier because its size
is larger; hence it is already included. If it equals \(S\), the
criterion explicitly permits it. Therefore no target include step is
pruned. Every such \(F\) is emitted. Each fixed decision sequence
encodes a different family, so it is emitted exactly once. QED.

**Corollary:** filtering these families by the test \(C_n(F)=e(F)\)
produces exactly one representative from each relabeling orbit of
full-support union-closed families. Each orbit has a unique least
integer encoding; that least member is itself generated by Lemma 2.

This prunes failed include branches rather than trying all
\(2^{2^n}\) set-system bitmasks. **It is not an output-sensitive
algorithm and has no guaranteed 10x complexity improvement.** The
naive orbit minimum still tests up to \(n!\) relabelings per family.
We distinguish this explicit construction from a faster canonical
*augmentation* algorithm, which remains an optimization target.

## Independent finite count audit

[OEIS A326906](https://oeis.org/A326906) lists the number of
union-closed families covering the entire \(n\)-element support.
The closure-pruned generator reproduces the **known** counts:

| Support size | Labeled full-support families | Orbit representatives |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 2 | 2 |
| 2 | 8 | 6 |
| 3 | 90 | 28 |
| 4 | 4,542 | 330 |
| 5 | 2,747,402 | Not exhaustively counted by the routine CI gate |

There is no contradiction with the existing 4,958-family/366-orbit
\(n=4\) mine: those totals allow families with **unused elements**.
There are \(\binom{4}{k}\) choices of exact \(k\)-element support,
so \(4(2)+6(8)+4(90)+4542=4958\).
Under relabeling of all four ground elements, orbit counts across
different support sizes add instead: \(2+6+28+330=366\).

The \(n=5\) labeled count is a full offline reproduction of a
published sequence, not a proof of the conjecture and not a novel
improvement. We do not claim to have exhausted the orbit classes
for \(n=5\) in CI. Their complete canonical list is a separate,
potentially expensive check.
