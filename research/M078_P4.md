# M078 — induced P₄ finishes the n ≤ 6 deck-invariant census

**Dated:** 2026-11-01. **Scope:** a finite separation statement on the
Burnside-certified graph census through 6 vertices. **Not claimed:** a proof of
the graph reconstruction conjecture, novelty over McKay’s computational
verification through 11 vertices, or separation at n = 7.

## The overlooked step

The certified mine [`deck_invariants`](../scripts/mines/deck_invariants.py)
already showed that Kelly’s degree sequence plus the triangle count separates
every graph through 5 vertices and leaves **20** colliding pairs at 6 vertices.
The campaign asked for a deck-readable invariant strictly stronger than those
two. Extending the census is refused: reconstruction is already known by
computer through at least 11 vertices.

## Deck-readable induced P₄

For n ≥ 5, every induced copy of P₄ in G uses four vertices and therefore
survives in exactly the n − 4 cards that delete a vertex outside that copy.
Consequently

$$
\#\{\text{induced }P_4\text{ in }G\}
= \frac{1}{n-4}\sum_v \#\{\text{induced }P_4\text{ in }G-v\}.
$$

The right-hand side is read from the deck alone. The independent checker in the
mine recomputes the left-hand side from the adjacency matrix and rejects any
mismatch.

## Finite separation theorem

On the complete unlabeled census for 3 ≤ n ≤ 6 (sizes 4, 11, 34, 156; Burnside
certified against OEIS A000088):

| Invariants | Separates through | Excess at n = 6 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Degree sequence | 4 | 54 |
| Degree + triangles | 5 | 20 |
| Degree + triangles + induced P₄ | **6** | **0** |

So every graph on at most six vertices is separated from every other by the
triple (degree sequence, triangle count, induced P₄ count), and each coordinate
is deck-readable for n ≥ 5.

At n = 7 the same triple still leaves 116 excess graphs among 1044; adding the
subgraph C₄ count leaves 36. Those collisions are the next structural target,
not a reason to enlarge the verified reconstruction range.
