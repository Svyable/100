## Exact statement

A finite loopless digraph on `n` vertices with minimum outdegree `r>=1`
must contain a directed cycle of length at most `ceil(n/r)`.
A two-way pair is a directed 2-cycle and automatically satisfies this bound,
so a counterexample may be assumed to be an **oriented** graph (no digons).

References: [EGRES open-problem statement](https://lemon.cs.elte.hu/egres/open/Caccetta-H%C3%A4ggkvist_conjecture);
[AI Mathematics Workshop summary](https://aimath.org/WWN/caccetta/caccetta.pdf).
Historical fixed-`r` results include `r=2,3,4,5`; a general
published directed-triangle threshold is approximately `0.3465n`.
For the density question, `n=18,r=6` is a natural next finite challenge
not directly covered by that numerical threshold (6/18 = 1/3).

**Status qualification:** recent externally posted manuscripts claim results for
the fixed-`r=6` problem. Their hypotheses, exact certificates, and independent
acceptance require specialist review; **do not** treat this instance as
independently confirmed open or claim novelty if their proof holds.
See the [external manuscript and proposed certificates](https://github.com/joshp123/solve-a-maths-problem-dont-care-which-make-no-mistakes)
as a *claim to audit*, not an accepted theorem.

## First substantial fixed-size target: (n=18, r=6)

For `n=18,r=6`, the desired bound is `ceil(18/6)=3`.
A hypothetical counterexample is an oriented digraph with minimum outdegree
6 and **no directed triangle**. An exact Boolean variable `x_uv` denotes
the arc `u->v`. The generated CNF contains:

| Clause family | Count | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| One negative 2-clause per unordered pair | 153 | No digons |
| Two negative 3-clauses per triple | 1,632 | No directed triangles |
| Each 12-subset of 17 outgoing candidates, per vertex | 111,384 | Minimum outdegree at least 6 |
| **Total** | **113,169** | **306 arc variables** |

The minimum-outdegree clauses are logically equivalent to the outdegree
bound: if a vertex has at most 5 outgoing arcs, there is a set of 12
other vertices with no outgoing arc, making one of the clauses false.
Conversely, at least 6 outgoing arcs intersect every 12-subset.

```sh
python3 scripts/ch_sat.py cnf --n 18 --r 6 --output /tmp/ch18-6.cnf
sha256sum /tmp/ch18-6.cnf
# expected: 32ae7c559724172e1213fbc9ee34053b2d02b791b2db47a6521482d2587f13e2
```

Generated CNF size: 5,125,375 bytes. A signed SAT assignment would be a
finite counterexample and must pass a separate graph verifier. An UNSAT
solver result is *not* a rigorous proof until independently verified via
a proof trace (e.g. LRAT with a trusted checker), including every
symmetry-breaking assumption. **No such SAT or UNSAT certificate has yet been
obtained in this repository.**

The generator tests prove semantic equivalence for every arc assignment
for small exact cases `(n=3,r=1)` and `(n=4,r=2)`, checking the CNF
against the independent graph-level definition. This tests the encoding
logic, not 18-vertex satisfiability.

## Proof-derived reduction: exact in/out regularity and root symmetry

A self-contained [six-vertex counting lemma and 18-vertex regularity theorem](M074_REGULARITY.md) show that any hypothetical counterexample at `(18,6)`, once reduced to exact outdegree six, must **also** have indegree six at every vertex. After relabeling a root, its six outgoing, six incoming and five nonadjacent neighbors can be pinned to fixed blocks without losing a counterexample.

The separate `scripts/ch_regularity.py` emits a reproducible stronger CNF with **3,834 variables and 15,139 clauses**, hash `16448ff34866b74e7b98ea38e599841363503de88590eca564ae29e2b9891d35`. This is an equisatisfiable structural reduction, **not** a SAT/UNSAT determination or a claim of originality. It leaves the frozen direct and compact CNF artifacts untouched. The next validation gate remains a solver trace independently checked against the exact generated CNF, with prior-art audit first.
