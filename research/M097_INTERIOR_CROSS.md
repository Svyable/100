## Precisely quantified theorem candidate

For any integer `k>=3` and integers `L_1,...,L_k>=2`, take two hubs `u,v`, the edge `uv`, and `k` otherwise internally vertex-disjoint `u-v` paths `P_i` of lengths `L_i`. Require `L_1,L_2>=4`. Add exactly one extra edge `xy`, with `x` on `P_1` and `y` on `P_2`, such that **each endpoint has distance at least two along its original path from both hubs**. No other edges are present. Let `m=sum L_i`, and let `c_G` be the complete shortest-path metric.

For the standard symmetric Held–Karp subtour LP (degree exactly two, nonnegative variables, every nontrivial cut at least two), and the metric Hamiltonian tour optimum, the proposed exact formulas are

```
SEP(c_G) = m
TSP(c_G) = m + (k mod 2) * min(1, min_i(L_i)-2).
```

Thus one *deep* cross-edge changes neither optimum relative to the direct-hub-edge-only chorded multitheta, even though it changes the metric. The maximum gap in this class is `12/11`, attained for `k=3`, lengths `(4,4,3)`, and the unique deep crossing of the two length-four paths. This is a **class-specific** gap, not a new global TSP lower bound. [The previously claimed chorded formula](M097_CHORDED_MULTITHETA.md) is used only for the explicit upper constructions, which are also specified here.

## All-orders proof

**1. Pair-cut lower bound, unaffected by the cross-edge.** For any feasible subtour point `x` on the complete metric, independently route each positive `x_{ab}` along any shortest `a-b` path in `G`, obtaining nonnegative original-edge loads `y_e` of the same total cost. For every vertex cut `S`, routing can only increase the cut load, hence `y(delta_G(S))>=x(delta(S))>=2`.

Split `P_1` at `x` and `P_2` at `y`. Their four arms have lengths at least two. Keep each other `P_i` as one segment, also of length at least two. Every *interior* vertex of each segment has graph degree exactly two. For any two distinct edges `e,f` of a segment, the nonempty contiguous set of vertices strictly between them has **exactly** `e,f` in its graph boundary, so `y_e+y_f>=2`. Sum over all pairs of edges in a segment of length `s>=2`: `(s-1)*sum_e y_e>=s(s-1)`, whence `sum_e y_e>=s`. The segments partition all `m` original long-path edges; the direct hub edge and the added cross-edge have nonnegative loads. Therefore **every feasible subtour point costs at least `m`**.

**2. Exact rational primal of cost `m`.** Set `alpha=2/k`, `gamma=(k-2)/(k(k-1))`. Give each original long-path edge incident with a hub weight `alpha`, every other original long-path edge weight one, and each pair of distinct first-internal vertices of different paths and each pair of distinct last-internal vertices weight `gamma` (add coincident contributions for length-two paths). All other metric pairs have zero weight. Every vertex has degree two. For a cut `S`, let `t_u` (respectively `t_v`) count the first (respectively last) internal path vertices lying opposite their corresponding hub. The total contribution of spokes and same-hub shortcuts at each hub is `F(t)=alpha*t+gamma*t*(k-t)`, with `F(0)=0`, `F(1)=1`, `F(k)=2` and `F(t)>=1` for every integer `1<=t<=k` by concavity. The remaining positive LP edges are the unit-weight interior path edges. If both counts are positive, the cut receives at least `1+1=2`. If either count is `k`, it receives at least two from that hub alone. If one count is zero and the other lies strictly between zero and `k`, at least one original interior path edge must cross: if the hubs lie on the same side, choose a path whose last vertex lies opposite `v`; if the hubs lie on opposite sides, choose a path whose last vertex lies on `v`'s side. In both cases its first and last internal vertices lie on opposite sides, and such a path cannot have length two, so a unit interior edge crosses. If both counts vanish and the hubs are opposite, every path must have length at least three (a length-two path would put its unique internal vertex on both hub sides), and every path contributes at least one unit interior crossing, giving `k>=3`. If both counts vanish and the hubs are on the same side, a nontrivial cut contains an internal vertex away from both ends of some path and has at least two unit interior crossings. These cases exhaust all nontrivial cuts and handle coincident first/last vertices of length-two paths by adding their shortcut contributions. The new cross-edge changes no cut in the **complete LP**, since the LP constraints depend on vertices and weights, not on the underlying graph. Every used original edge still has metric cost one. Every used same-hub shortcut still costs exactly two: the only new route through `xy` has at least three edges between first-internal vertices of different paths (and similarly for last-internal vertices), whereas the route through the hub has length two. Therefore the primal has cost `m`, proving `SEP=m`.

**3. Integral tour lower bound and parity.** Any metric tour expands into a connected spanning Eulerian edge-multigraph `M` on `G` of the same length. The segment pair-cut inequalities apply to its integer edge multiplicities. Hence every tour costs at least `m`. If `k` is even, taking each original long path once and omitting `uv,xy` gives a connected Eulerian multigraph of length `m`. If `k` is odd but some path has length two, take all other long paths once, double one edge of that length-two path and omit its other edge; again omit `uv,xy`. This is connected, spans every vertex, has even degrees and costs `m`.

Suppose now `k` is odd and **every** `L_i>=3`. Assume for contradiction that a connected Eulerian multigraph has total cost `m`. Every segment must attain its lower bound exactly, and both `uv` and `xy` have multiplicity zero. For a segment of length at least three, pair inequalities with equality of the total force every edge multiplicity to be exactly one. For a segment of length two, the only nonnegative *integer* equality possibilities are `(1,1),(2,0),(0,2)`. Every internal degree-two vertex forces its two incident edge multiplicities to have equal parity. At a crossed vertex, `xy` is unused, so parity also agrees across the two incident arms. Thus all edges of each original `P_i` have one common parity. An uncrossed path of length at least three cannot be even at equality. A crossed path with an arm of length at least three cannot be even either. The sole remaining even possibility is a crossed path of length four split into two length-two arms: each arm then has one zero edge, disconnecting the nonempty middle section between the two zero edges from the rest of `M` (the cross-edge is unused). This contradicts connectedness. Consequently every original path is odd, and the degree of hub `u` is odd because `k` is odd and `uv` is unused. Contradiction.

Therefore `TSP>=m+1` in this case. The connected Eulerian multigraph containing every long path once and the direct edge `uv` once has cost `m+1`. The integral formula follows.

## Quantitative LP stability and optimal-face exclusion

The same proof yields an all-orders inequality stronger than the optimum-value formula. For any feasible subtour point of objective `m+epsilon` with `epsilon>=0`, let `B_xy` consist of complete-metric pairs admitting **at least one** shortest route through the added cross-edge `xy`. Then

```
sum_{ab in B_xy} x_ab <= epsilon.
```

The same inequality holds separately for the set `B_uv` of pairs admitting a shortest route through the direct hub edge. In particular, **every subtour optimum gives zero weight to every metric pair admitting a geodesic through either added edge**, even when shortest paths are tied.

Proof: route every positive metric pair along any shortest original-graph path. Write `delta_A=sum_{e in A} y_e-|A|>=0` for each of the disjoint original-path segments `A` from step 1. The exact cost decomposition is `epsilon=y_uv+y_xy+sum_A delta_A`. To bound the mass on `B_xy`, independently choose a shortest route through `xy` for each pair in that set. This gives `y_xy>=sum_{ab in B_xy}x_ab` and `y_xy<=epsilon`. Repeat with a separate route selection for `uv`. No claim is made that both bounds can be added without accounting for pairs admitting both kinds of routes.

For each segment `A` of length `s>=3`, its pair-cut inequalities and `delta_A<=epsilon` additionally give `1-epsilon/(s-2) <= y_e <= 1+epsilon` for every segment edge `e` under **any** shortest-route selection. Indeed, writing `z_e=y_e-1`, every pair satisfies `z_e+z_f>=0`; a negative deviation `-t` forces all other deviations at least `t`, hence `delta_A>=(s-2)t`, while a positive deviation `t` forces `delta_A>=t`. At optimum every edge on every segment of length at least three has routed load exactly one. The length-two exception is real: only the sum of its two loads is forced to two.

This stability statement is author-derived and has **not** been independently reviewed or checked for historical priority.

## Reproduction, adversarial checks, failed hypotheses

`python3 -m scripts.m097_interior_cross --check` reconstructs the graph and BFS metric, verifies the explicit primal in exact `Fraction` arithmetic against every degree and nontrivial subtour cut, and independently computes the tour optimum with a subset dynamic program. **83** parameter-and-position instances of at most 12 vertices passed, including asymmetric path lengths, length-two uncrossed paths, odd/even path counts and both deep-cross endpoint positions. A separate author-written, independently formulated SciPy/HiGHS full-cut LP optimization matched `SEP=m` on **all 83** cases (floating-point cross-check only; not committed as an independent mathematical review). Additional exact tour DP probes included `(5,5,5)`, `(6,6,4)`, and larger even-path cases. The test rejects a tampered LP witness with spurious hub-edge mass.

**Failed stronger hypothesis / sharpness of depth condition.** Merely requiring one endpoint to be deep is not sufficient for tour invariance. On `(L_1,L_2,L_3)=(4,4,3)`, put the cross-edge between path positions `(2,3)` (the second endpoint is adjacent to `v`). Label the paths `0-2-3-4-1`, `0-5-6-7-1`, `0-8-9-1`, with direct edge `0-1` and cross-edge `3-7`. The metric tour `0-2-5-6-7-3-4-1-9-8-0` has cost **11**, below the deep-class prediction `m+1=12`. An exact subset DP gives optimum 11. Moving the crossing to `3-6` restores the deep hypothesis and the computed tour optimum 12. This is a falsifier of the *broader* claim, not of the restricted theorem.
