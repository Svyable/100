# M075: a candidate sharp seven-element triple-local theorem

**Status (2026-11-01):** A complete **computer-assisted finite exclusion candidate**, with an exact 17-size SAT reduction, DRAT refutation, independently implemented DRAT/LRAT checking, and a published 19-set positive witness. This is **not** an accepted new theorem until external mathematicians audit the reduction, scope, certificate, and prior art. It does not settle Frankl's conjecture. See [issue #95](https://github.com/Svyable/100/issues/95) and the [scoped contribution claim #133](https://github.com/Svyable/100/issues/133).

## Precise candidate statement

Let F be a finite union-closed family of **distinct subsets of a fixed seven-element universe**. Assume:

- The empty set belongs to F.
- The exact set T={0,1,2} belongs to F (other sets may be of size one or two, or of any size).
- For each t in T, strictly fewer than half the members of F contain t.

**Claim:** |F| >= 19. The example of van der Hout–Roos (2026, Figure 1), with |F|=19 and triple frequencies (9,9,9), attains the bound; it has abundant elements outside T, so is **not** a Frankl counterexample.

**Important scope distinction:** We do *not* assume T is the smallest nonempty member of F, or even that F has minimum nonempty set size three. A September 2026 [unreviewed community report](https://mathdb.com/p/355601/the-union-closed-sets-conjecture) claims a 19-set bound when the triple *is* globally minimum. The present finite restriction differs; originality and historical equivalence are not established. Both statements concern a false *triple-local strengthening*, not the original Frankl conjecture.

## Step 1 — an elementary structural lemma (human-checkable)

**Lemma.** In any union-closed F, if T={a,b,c} belongs to F and each of a,b,c is *strictly rare*, no nonempty proper subset of T belongs to F.

For a singleton {a} in F, the map U -> U union {a} from sets U omitting a into sets containing a is injective: it simply adds a. Thus f(a) >= |F|/2, contradiction.

For a pair {a,b} in F, partition the family into D (containing neither), A (only a), C (only b) and B (both). Union with {a,b} injects D into B, so |B| >= |D|. Then

    f(a) + f(b) - |F|
      = (|A|+|B|) + (|C|+|B|) - (|D|+|A|+|C|+|B|)
      = |B| - |D| >= 0.

Strict rarity of both a and b implies f(a)+f(b) < |F|, a contradiction. The other singletons/pairs are symmetric. **QED.**

This is an elementary consequence, not by itself a new discovery; it licenses exactly **six** negative membership literals for the proper nonempty subsets of T. No unproved symmetry-breaking or "global smallest triple" assumption is used.

## Step 2 — one CNF covers every size 2..18

For each of the 2^7 = 128 possible subsets S of {0,...,6}, introduce x_S, true iff S in F. Include x_empty and x_T. Every pair A,B has the union clause

    (not x_A) OR (not x_B) OR x_(A union B).

The singleton/pair lemma supplies the six unit exclusions. A definitional prefix threshold counter g(i,j) is true iff at least j of the first i membership variables are true, with recurrence

    g(i,j) iff g(i-1,j) OR (x_i AND g(i-1,j-1)),

and analogues for each t in T, counting the selected sets containing t. The recurrence is encoded by **four clauses** per auxiliary gate; its auxiliary values are uniquely determined by the 128 membership bits.

For each integer k in 2..18 introduce a selector y_k that implies:
- cardinality >= k and cardinality NOT >= k+1 (exactly k members);
- frequency(t) NOT >= floor((k-1)/2)+1, for each t in T.

Finally require OR(y_2,...,y_18). Exactly one k can be selected by a feasible assignment, and every hypothetical F satisfying the stated conditions with |F|<=18 has a satisfying extension. Conversely a satisfying CNF model would be a real F contradicting the claim. The full finite property therefore reduces to **one UNSAT instance**, not 17 unconnected solver reports.

**Independent semantics tests:** all 256 possible families on a three-element universe at caps 2..8; randomized n=4 families at caps 5,8,12,16; positive n=7 published witness at cap 19 and negative check at cap 18. The tests use direct union and frequency operations independently of the counter circuits.

## Limitations / genuinely next steps

This is a precise, computationally checked **finite candidate**, not an unrestricted theorem. The author's own independent tool replay does **not** substitute for external specialist review. The SAT-to-mathematics correspondence, certificate tooling, and originality all require separate review before admission to a new-results ledger. The existing bounded size-17 and size-18 *unaccelerated* trials remain historically **unknown**; this later proof-carrying calculation uses sound redundant constraints and a different capped CNF, so the records are not retroactively rewritten.

For a next theorem, determine whether the 19 lower bound holds without fixing the universe at seven, or derive a short human proof of the finite bound from a fiber decomposition. Those are genuinely stronger/different questions and require fresh evidence. In either case, do not mark M075 or the full Frankl conjecture resolved.
